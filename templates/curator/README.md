# `curator@2.1.0`

The window of one model, owned in one place, with a ledger of every call. The record of the conversation, the wall and its blocks, is append-only and never deleted; for each call this hive cuts out of it the window the model behind it reads, by that model's numbers: the soft and the hard line of its window, its input, cached and cache-write prices and its price ladder (The window per target, The price of the window). A target differs only by its profile and the knobs of the instance. What the window no longer holds stays reachable through the history tools. Trust is the goal and latency the variable. The aim is that every window starts from a whole day or session and is cut only when a hard limit is crossed (the window, the soft line, the target's `window_target`, the cost rule with its price ladder); where that limit lies is to be measured, not set, and today the horizons `task` and `all` cut by other measures than the day. Why it is built this way: [A window cut to the model](../../docs/why/curator.md). Contract tables follow.

Since 1.1.0 ([#892](https://github.com/mmeyerlein/meclaw/issues/892), [#893](https://github.com/mmeyerlein/meclaw/issues/893), [#895](https://github.com/mmeyerlein/meclaw/issues/895), [#896](https://github.com/mmeyerlein/meclaw/issues/896)) the window follows a role (`role`: `talky`,
`consult`, `coding`, `research`), foreign words and tool results stand behind a short id
`[#<12 hex>]` the model can release or pin through the `window` section of its answer, another hive
can pin a text into the window (`in_pin`), and the hive answers the collector's menu question itself
(`./schemas`). Three cells joined it: `./history` answers the `history_*` tools out of the ledger,
`./push` builds the memory leg's question and looks a `gap` up after the answer, and `./handover`
hands a new session a note on the last one and a renewed duplex call a block of the recent
conversation. Lanes joined the boundary, so it is the second digit.

Since 1.1.1 ([#904](https://github.com/mmeyerlein/meclaw/issues/904)) the cache clock is one standing order under a fixed id per hive (per round since GH #943),
re-armed on every call (`add` with `rearm`), so the timer keeps one row for it instead of one
per call. No lane moved; a promise is repaired, so it is the third digit.

**The system part of a turn stands (GH #1298).** A brain answers one turn in several calls, one per tool round, and a provider that signs its thinking binds every block to the prefix it was made under (`system`, `tools`, the earlier messages): a replay under another prefix is refused (the native Messages wire answers 400). The leaf carries no pins, because the window shows every valid pin already. It stands for the session and falls at the first rebuild after it of the round its audience names (GH #943). A renewed live session (`in_renewed`) sees no system part of this hive, so its block carries it all: the newest `renew_rounds` rounds as text, the summary, the pins, the open commitments (pins with `source` `commitment`) and the topic, at once and without a model. The block opens on a fixed head that names a renewed session of the same conversation, never a call, and leaves as a `sidecar` with `live` and `renewal_n`; the `handover` mark and `renewed_last:<session>` carry `live` in their value (GH #1482). Open commitments stay empty until a hive pins them through `in_pin`; no parent draws that door yet. Every block is at most its share of the window (the window rule below), cut by rank: pins and commitments, then the topic, then the turns, the summary last. The pack family `handover` is the identity pusher's and is never touched; this leaf is `history.handover`, owner `curator`. Since GH #1225 every row the handover keeps -- the parked note, the leaf's slot, `handover_audience`, `handover_for`, `renewed_last:<session>`, `summarizer_input_soft` -- is written in one step (`insert` with `replace` on its key, a block `if_absent` on its hash), so a refused write leaves the old value, never none. Its reads name what they look for, with no count: the newest summary the round may see and the wall above its cover, the newest `topic` mark that says something (however many quiet turns came since), the `handover` marks of the one session in question. A read the ledger refused is never "no rows": the step reads once more, and refused again it ends loudly -- a `store_error` mark, the code in the header -- with no note, no leaf for a returning session, and a renewal block that carries its head only. Whether a session is prepared is decided by a `prepared` mark read from the ledger, never by what the child sent.

## The window per target

**The handle (D58, GH #1483).** Every curate has a target: the name under which the composite wires the model cell the window is cut for, unique per curator. The policy takes it from the lead's verdict (`target`), else from the curate's `hop.target`. A composite without a lead sets `hop.target` itself, on its edge to the curator or in the cell that sends the curate. There is no built-in target. A curate that names none is cut for the one target the curator holds a row of, else on the knobs of the instance, and leaves a `target_unknown` mark -- except a call that leaves the composite (way `away`): it has no target by contract and leaves no such mark.

**The profile.** A model cell with `package_line` reports its package on the lane `in_catalog` and its profile in the body slot `window_profile` beside it (the llm param of that name, `docs/cell-types.md`). It reports again after every change of either and when the curator asks (`catalog_ask`). The curator holds row and profile per target and keeps no table of its own. The fields:

**Precedence.** A value of the profile holds. A value it does not name comes from the knob set on the instance, else from the shipped value. The values a call's cut reads (`keep_recent`, `notes_share`, `short_ids`, `identity_budget`, `state_lines`, `broadcast_mode`, `window_target`) come from the profile of the call's target. The values the tap of an answer and a rebuild read belong to the wall, which all targets share, so each has one source: the reference target. That is the call's target when it holds its tree (`system` `delta`), else the one reported target that does. With none or several, and the call going to none of them, the knobs of the instance hold and the call leaves a `target_unknown` mark (`missing` "wall"). A composite with several tree-holding targets and further ones therefore sets the wall's values on the instance. A rebuild counts with the reference target's row, and the self-summary (`summarizer_self`) runs through that target.

**A target without a profile.** A call to a target the curator holds no profile of is cut like a bare curator: the shipped fields, but holding its tree (`system` `delta`, all tools, no slot of its own), and the knobs of the instance; it leaves a `target_unknown` mark. Beside the call, never instead of it, the curator asks the cell once per session (`catalog_ask`). A colony with a registry pushes every cell its package after birth, so the case stays rare. The fallback knobs of the window sizes (`context_window`, `input_soft_fallback`, `input_soft_fallback_row`) hold only until a row is reported.

**The label and the answer.** Every call to a model leaves on the one route `model_call` and names its target in `hop.target` (Routes); the composite delivers by that name, and the curator names no cell. The answer brings its target back in the context key `curator_target`, which the composite's edges set; the curator reads it where the call's own projection names none. An answer without either counts as the answer of the target that closes the turn.

**Upgrading from `role`, `quick_tools` and `quick_instructions_slot`.** The three knobs of `./policy` are gone. A set that set `role` writes that set's values (above) into the profile of its target, the `window_profile` of the llm cell. `quick_tools` becomes the profile's `tools` `scoped` with `allow` [the tools]. `quick_instructions_slot` "1" becomes the profile's `instructions` `{"slot": .., "text": .., "only": true}`.

The window is the system part (slots, `pinned.*`, stubs) + the wall after the plan's cover, each block as the plan shows it + the running round. A rebuild (cold cache, a line of the model's package -- below --, or without a package `tokens_prompt` over `compress_at` of the model's own window) makes the plan: segments by `horizon`, their form by `tiers` (`raw`, `summary`, `none`), the newest `keep_recent` rounds and the newest round always raw and, since GH #1427, the youngest rounds that fit the raw token budget `keep_recent_tokens` (example set `talky`: 80000; null or 0 = off), across session and day boundaries, capped at the rebuild's aim less the system part -- both measured as the window sends the rounds, after the garbage collection of `gc` "1", a collected tool result or an expired transient block at its one-line form and an answer cut at its sidecar block at the text sent (GH #1434); the plan's own estimate against the aim counts the same form --, the model's pins raw. The running round always goes out whole -- the person's words and every call/result pair -- and a cut before it never separates a tool call from its result, in older rounds neither: a target whose profile names `keep_recent` and does not hold its tree (`system` other than `delta`; talky's fast targets: 4) counts it in elements before the running round, and the cut moves back to the call of every result it would orphan (GH #1347: counted over the whole window, a round of three calls side by side lost the question and two calls). Over `rebuild_to` of the usable window it shrinks old tool results (outside `keep_rounds`), then stubs tools unused for `stub_tools_after` rounds, then gives the summary up. The aim of a rebuild is a guide value by the plan's own measure (the characters of the blocks over the rate), not a limit of the finished call: the call adds its envelope on top (JSON keys, id prefixes, the new message), and the hard limits are checked by the llm cell on the real call. Between two rebuilds only the end of the window moves.

**No window of a role's own (R-IG-1, GH #1085, since 1.11.3).** The usable window is the catalog row of the answering model: `input_soft`, which the llm cell stamps on every answer (GH #1037), cut only to the model's own reported window. Until 1.11.2 a role capped it (`quality_cap`: talky 120000, consult 80000, coding 70000, research 80000 tokens, GH #892) -- a talky under luna (`input_soft` 250000) kept 120000, handed that to every producer and, on an answer without the row's limits, rebuilt at 60000. Whether a model reads a smaller window well is a question of its catalog row, never a knob of this hive.

**Short ids.** With `short_ids` on, somebody else's words and a tool result begin with `[#<12 hex>] `, the first 12 hex digits of the wall block's hash; the model's own answers and tool calls never do. The model names ids in its `window` section: `release` shows the block as `[#<id> released — history_read("#<id>")]` from the next rebuild on, `pin` keeps it raw below the cover. Identity, persona, instructions and the round the section was said in stay; an unknown id is ignored and said. A block with `valid_until` passed at the plan's time shows as `[#<id> expired]`. A model may quote an id in another bracket, a footnote mark `[^<id>]` and its definition line among them; whatever leaves toward a reader (`./push`, `./handover`, and the parent's splitter) loses it by form, the same one expression in every copy ([#1345](https://github.com/mmeyerlein/meclaw/issues/1345)). The ledger keeps every block byte for byte; a form the window shows is a block of kind `view`.

**Window plan per round (GH #943).** `./policy` keeps one window plan per round, not one per hive: with one plan, a rebuild one round started set the cut, the shrunk results and the stubs of every other round's window. A round's key `round_key` is the first 12 hex digits of the SHA-256 of the canonical round (the JSON array sorted, without duplicates, as `audience_set` carries it); a call without a round has the key of `[]`, and `["*"]` is a round of its own. Under it `state` holds `window_plan:<rk>`, the rebuild claim `rebuild_running:<rk>` (one rebuild per round at a time), `last_call:<rk>`, `armed_call:<rk>`, `actions_pending:<rk>`, `pending:summary:<rk>` and `summary_audience:<rk>`, and `./intake` hands a call on with the plan of its own round. The summary slot is `history.summary:<rk>`, shown to its round as the leaf `history.summary` and to no other round. The cache clock holds one order per round (`schedule_id` = uuid5 of the policy's namespace and `cache:<rk>`), so a call of one round never re-arms another round's order. `state` `window_plans` maps each round key to the time of its last rebuild; every rebuild stamps its own round and deletes the state of the rounds idle for longer than `round_plan_idle_s` (default 2592000 s, 30 days), then of the oldest beyond `max_round_plans` (default 64) -- never its own; what a dropped round owns falls with its plan (GH #949): its state rows including `summary_audience:<rk>` and its summary slot, deleted only after the registry write that drops it was won, row by row against the value read. The first call of a returning round whose newest visible summary is its own lays its leaf, slot and binding afresh from that `summaries` row; a cache order of the round that is still armed falls too -- the timer is told `remove` for the round's id (it marks the row and keeps it), an order that struck already gets none, and a strike before the removal finds no `last_call:<rk>`. A handover leaf bound to no audience reaches no round and falls at the next rebuild of any round. The ledger declares `state.key` unique (`state_key`), so the first write of a round's key is an insert only one writer wins, and a lost registry write is read and put again (at most three times). The bare keys of the one plan per hive (`window_plan`, `rebuild_running`, `last_call`, `armed_call`, `actions_pending`, `pending:summary`, `summary_audience`, a bare `history.summary` slot) are neither read nor moved: every round starts its plan afresh. `round_key`, `round_canon`, `own_slots` and `prune_plans` are pure functions like those below.

**The plan of building blocks (GH #1448, D38 C2).** Beside the rebuild's keys the round's plan holds `blocks`, the window as a list of building blocks `{id, kind, prio, origin, tokens, audience, valid_until}`, and `blocks_to`, the newest wall `seq` it covers. `id` is `<kind>:<short id of the origin>`; `kind` one of `words` (the person's), `answer`, `tool_call`, `tool_result`, `recall`, `brief`, `note` (an advice or delegation row), `push` (a gap's find or the candidates `./push` handed on, `call_addendum_`/`call_candidates_`), `stub` (a row a rebuild released, shortened or saw run out); `origin` `{source: wall, hash}`, the brief's `{source: summary, hash}`; `tokens` the neutral estimate (three bytes a token, never a catalog row's rate or window -- stage 1 is model free); `prio` from `block_prio`; `audience` the canonical round. A rebuild writes the blocks of the rows its window still shows and the brief in front; `render` sends a wall row exactly when a block names it. Each call takes the plan its turn was parked with and appends a block for every wall row the plan does not hold yet (the person's words, the legs of this call, a row stamped below `blocks_to` after the plan was written); the call's plan rides in `window_est:<call>` with the plan text it was built on, and the answer's tap appends the answer's blocks and writes the plan by compare-and-set on that text -- a rebuild in between wins. A plan of blocks only reads as no plan to every step that reads the rebuild's keys: the window is the window of before, byte for byte. Each call leaves one row in `snapshots` (the ids of all the blocks it sends with `prio` and `tokens`: the plan's and, built in the call and never kept in the plan, the system part and identity as `frame`, the state line as `state`, every other note as `note`), under its round; a round keeps its newest `snapshot_keep`. With `recall_in_wall` on (GH #1463) the plan also holds `left_out`, the wall hashes of the recall pairs a call decided to leave out, and `ahead`, the blocks the tap planned ahead for an answer until the wall has booked it; a recall block that was decided and stays carries `decided` `kept`.

**The window as sent (`snapshot_window`, GH #1462, D58).** The plan and the cut say what a call was meant to hold, by block id. With `snapshot_window` on, the same row also carries `sent`: canonical JSON `[{hash, kind, form, bytes}]`, one entry per element in the order it was sent. First the leaves of the system part (`kind` `frame`), then the identity, then the messages (`kind` the block kind of the plan), last the tool menu (`kind` `tools`, the part of the call's `tool_scope` that makes the menu: `allow` when the scope names one, `deny` and `hint` when not empty; absent when the call names no scope). `hash` is the sha256 of the bytes as sent and names a row of `blocks` whose `body` is exactly those bytes: a leaf as `{path, text}`, a message as its element, the menu as that JSON object. `bytes` is the length of that body in UTF-8. `form` says how the element left: `raw` (the content whole, also behind an id prefix, a frame or the notes block), `released`, `stub` and `expired` (the one-line forms of a block outside the running round), `cut` (an answer without its over-sized sidecar block), `built` (made in the cut and held by no slot and no wall row: the frame line, the bridge instruction, the state line, a note). For a one-line form the hash names the line, not the wall block; the wall block is found through the id in `body` and `cut`. The state line and each note are sent inside the message that carries the notes block. Each stands as an entry of its own (`kind` `state` or `note`, `form` `built`, body `{text}`) right before that message, and the message follows whole. The bytes of these pieces are therefore contained in the bytes of the carrying message: whoever sums sizes leaves the `built` entries out. A later call carries only the families of the system part that moved, and the model's cell holds the rest from the calls before. `sent` therefore lists the whole tree the model holds for the call, and every entry of the system part carries one more key, `carried` (a bool): `true` = this call carried the leaf to the model's cell, `false` = the cell held it already. On a first call and on a bridge every leaf is `carried`. A call whose cut sends no system part at all (its target answers under its own) lists no leaf. Only the leaves of the system part carry it; the identity (also `kind` `frame`), the state line, the notes, the messages and the tool menu have no such key. From `sent` and `blocks` alone the window can be put together again byte for byte: the entries with `carried` `true` give the system part of the message to the model's cell, all entries of the system part together give the system part the model answers under. Whoever asks whether a piece of context was there reads all entries, never only the carried ones. The bridge is a call of its own and lists its own window in its own row. The list is made from what the cut already holds, with no read of the ledger; blocks the ledger does not hold yet are written in the call's own bundle, once per content. With `recall_in_wall` on, the call that decides to leave a recall pair out (GH #1463) lists the pair too, behind the messages: one entry for the result and one for its `tool_call`, each with the form `cut` and one more key, `why` (`recall_in_wall`). Such an entry names the block of the wall, the bytes that were NOT sent; an entry with `why` is no part of the window, and only the deciding call lists it. With `recall_skip_sure` on (GH #1466) the call that leaves a fetched recall pair out for its turn's stamp lists it the same way, with `why` `recall_skip_sure`. Off (the default) the row carries no `sent` and no block more is written; on or off the model is sent the same bytes. `sent` lists the system part by path; the provider gets it in the order of the llm cell (`system_order`, the families it names first, the rest alphabetically), so a reader that puts the system part together byte for byte orders the entries by that rule.

**The cut per target model (GH #1450, D38 C3b).** Stage 1 (the plan) knows no model; stage 2 cuts the call's blocks to the window of the model cell the call goes to (its target, `hop.target`). Its numbers come from that cell's catalogue row (`catalog:<cell>`, from `in_catalog`) before the tap's copy of the last answer header, and from the tap's state without a row, as before: `input_soft`, `input_hard` (the guard line, `HARD_HEADROOM` of it), `chars_per_token` (the estimate of the call and the rate the rebuild of the brain plans at). The cut orders and frames the blocks, applies the guard, the quick way's scope, the system families and the target's tool scope, and never adds a block: every id it sends is one of the call's, every block it leaves out it names with its reason (`overflow_cut`, `quick_scope`, `not_shown`). The call's snapshot row carries it in `cut`: `{target, model, ids, dropped, budget: {soft, target, hard}, cache_mode, cache_ttl_s, cache_at, tools, cut_ms}` -- `cache_at` the id of the last block before the running round when the row names a cache mode (the snapshot only; the wire's breakpoints stay the llm cell's), `tools` the names the call's tool scope allows. `ids` stand in the order the call sends them. The row's `input_soft` is cut to the window the target's own model reported (`catalog:<target>:context_window`), like the tap's copy -- a target that never reported a window is not cut, and another target's window never cuts it; a field the row lacks is the tap's. A side call is a call of its own: cut here as well, for the `target` of its `side` entry, with its own snapshot row. `cut_ms` measures the sum of the cut's own functions (budget, guard and scope, tools, system families, the record) and nothing of the window build between them; the battery reads it as the cut's share of the policy's `handle_ms`, never as the whole station. A new model is one catalogue row, no change here. A call to a cell whose row the policy does not hold asks that cell once per session (lane `catalog_ask`), beside the call.

**Reading the round (`in_read`, GH #949).** An installed app reads the ledger of its round on `in_read`, answered on `read` with `hop.read_tag` echoed: `what` `turns` pages the wall rows its round may see (oldest first from the cursor `since`, `limit` 20 by default and 100 at most, `next` empty at the end, within a tenth of the reader's window, `hop.input_soft` of the read -- never the window this hive serves, which is another model's -- every row whole when the read names none; a first row larger than that is cut with the mark of the window rule below and flagged `cut`), `sessions` lists the sessions with their last time, `snapshots` pages the plan snapshots of the calls of its round (oldest first from `since`, `limit` as above; each row `seq`, `call_id`, `session_id`, `turn_id`, `at` and `blocks`, the call's block ids with their `prio` and `tokens` as the policy stored them, GH #1448, and `cut`, the call's cut as an object, `null` in a row from before, GH #1450). The round is `context.audience_set` and nothing else, matched through `covers` like every reader of the ledger: a read without a round, or with `[]`, is answered `missing_audience` and reads nothing, a row reaches the read only when its audience holds the round, and a failed store read is `store_error`, never an empty page. The way in is `install_app` `reads`, which names the app's cell and the brain by `hop.organ`; a run the timer wakes carries no round and is refused.

**The price of the window (GH #1038).** A model's limits differ per model, and a cached window is not free: at a cached price of 10 % of the input price, a 500k window costs on every turn what 50k fresh tokens cost. The llm cell stamps the answering model's catalogue values on every answer (GH #1037): `input_soft` and `input_hard` in tokens, `cost_in` and `cost_cached_in` in cents per million tokens. With `input_soft` on the tap they replace the role's own numbers -- `compress_at` of the model's window orders nothing: a rebuild is due at `input_soft` (`curate_mark` `soft`), over `input_hard` (`hard`; the llm cell refuses such a call itself), and under soft when keeping the cached rest costs more over `amortise_turns` turns than one rebuild, `(window - aim) * cost_cached_in * N > aim * cost_in` (`cost`). The aim of a `soft` or `hard` rebuild is `input_soft` times `rebuild_to` / `compress_at` (the share of the window W11 made soft keeps its proportion), `input_soft` itself for a `hard` rebuild of a role without one; a `cost` rebuild aims lower, at `input_soft` times `rebuild_to` -- with the soft share as its aim the rule would need a cached price above 23 % (talky) of the input price and never fire, with this one it is due under soft from 0.7 (talky, consult) or 0.5 (coding, research) of `input_soft` at a cached price of 10 % and ten turns. Without a price ladder the rule is conservative: it counts the rebuild in full, never the cached part the rebuilt call itself still reads, at the row's cache-write price (`cost_cache_write`, GH #1464: a rebuild writes the whole rebuilt window into the cache, whatever the shape of the price list) and at `cost_in` when the row names none. With a ladder on the tap (GH #1431, `price_tiers`, the base step first) it prices each side at its own step: the held window is read at the cached price of the step it falls in, the rebuilt window at the step its aim falls in, and the rebuild is counted at that step's cache-write price (`cost_cache_write`; its input price when the list names none) -- and up to the target's `window_target` the price never rebuilds (nor in the hold over it), above it `target` does, and without a ladder the rule of before holds. `turn_cost(window, cached, limits)` prices a turn for the rule and for `cost_cents` alike; without a ladder the numbers of before hold, byte for byte. Worked example: `cost_in` 10 cents per million, cached 10 % of it, a 500k window rebuilt to 100k -- keeping it costs 0.4 cents a turn more, the rebuild 1 cent once, so it pays back on the third turn. Every turn keeps `{call, curate_mark, window_tokens, cached_tokens, cost_cents, input_soft, input_hard}` under `state` `curate:<rk>`, and a turn with the package's values writes the same as a `marks` row of kind `curate`; the clock order of such a rebuild carries the mark as its `reason` and as `curate_mark`. The window is measured, never summed: after a `length` continuation the llm cell reports `tokens_prompt` as the sum of every call of the answer, so the package's rule takes the smaller of that and the policy's own projection of the call (every system leaf and every message, kept as `state` `window_est:<call>` until the tap -- a call no tap answers loses it to the next call of its round, and the round's pointer `est_call:<rk>` falls with the round --, estimated like the llm cell estimates a request: UTF-8 bytes of every string and key / 3, an inline image 1024 tokens). And because the cell refuses over `input_hard` on that estimate, `hard` is also due when the estimate of the next window -- this call plus its answer -- reaches 0.9 of `input_hard`; the tenth is headroom for what the projection does not see (the provider request's framing, the next turn, a tool result). Without `input_soft` (an old package) the rule of before holds and the mark is `none`.

**The garbage collection of a rebuild (GH #1430).** With `gc` "1" (talky) every rebuild collects, not only one over its aim: the tool results of all but the last `keep_rounds` tool rounds take their one-line form (class `shrunk_tools`); a `memory_recall` result (the pushed one or one the model called) that shares one row with a younger result the window keeps raw takes it too (`dup_recall`; a row is a fact or said line as the memory hive renders it -- at an indent of exactly two and no parenthesised caveat, compared by its bytes; heads, caveats and replies are no rows); the inner `(advice)`/`(delegation)` rows of a delegation whose round deadline has passed take it (`scaffold`; `gc_scaffold` "0" keeps this class off alone, the companion's answer of that round always stays). A result that failed never shrinks and the window shows it whole, whatever a plan names: the rebuild reads the verdict `./intake` wrote beside the wall row when it filed the result (`wall.failed`, GH #1468 -- an error object as the text, or an `error_code` in the result's header, the collector's `tool_errors`, so a failure told in prose counts); a row filed before the field holds none and is judged by its text alone, as before (an empty text or an error object); the person's words never shrink; every form keeps `history_read`. With `gc` "0" tool results shrink only over the aim, as before. Every rebuild writes one mark `gc` `{call, classes: {shrunk_tools, dup_recall, transient, released, scaffold}, tool_share, recall_rows}`: the count per class (`released` the blocks the model released, `transient` the expired ones), `tool_share` the share of tool-result characters in the window the plan leaves (0..1), `recall_rows` the rows (as above) of the newest recall result it keeps raw. With `recall_in_wall` on (GH #1463) the class `dup_recall` also takes a whole block: a recall result of an earlier round whose every quote stands raw in a turn the window keeps above the cover takes its one-line form, by the rule a call decides a new block by; the rows of a result are then compared with a quote over several lines as one row. A block a call kept stays in every warm call and leaves only here. The `stats` door counts it as kind `gc` (GH #1426).

**Collecting when the cache ran out (`gc_on_cold`, `cold_to`, GH #1464).** A provider's cache lives for minutes; once it ran out, the next turn pays the whole window at the input price anyway, so that pause is the cheapest moment to make the window smaller. With `gc_on_cold` "1" and `gc` "1" a rebuild for an expired cache (trigger `cache`: the lane `in_cache_expired`, or the clock refusing the order) under its aim only collects: the classes of the garbage collection above, deterministic and without a model, while the cover and the summary of the plan before stay as they were. It never takes the person's words, the companion's answers or a result that failed, and it never touches a turn that is still running or a tool round that is still open: a strike whose call is no longer the newest of its round -- another call went out since, the cache is warm again -- lapses. A collection that takes nothing leaves the window and the ledger as they are: no plan, no mark. One that takes something writes the one `gc` mark and a `curate` mark with `curate_mark` `cold` and `cost_cents` 0 (no model was called, no `window_tokens`), so the figures `gc` and `rebuild` of `stats` see it. Over its aim the rebuild of before runs, with the summarizer. The aim is `cold_to`, a share of the target's `window_target` (`0.45` = 45 %); empty (as shipped) is the aim the order of the cache inherited from the last answer, and a target without a `window_target` only collects. The knobs act the same for every target; no profile carries them, they are the instance's. The changed prefix of such a rebuild is the rebuild's own, no prefix break.

**A catalogue row that comes late changes the window only with a rebuild (GH #1497).** The first call of a new colony leaves before any model cell has told its catalogue row; the question for it goes beside the call (`catalog_ask`), and the row comes home before the next call. Cut by it, that next call began with other bytes: the words got their short id, the identity moved, the slot another target names left the system part -- a prefix break without a rebuild. So a target is cut by the catalogue rows its window was first cut by on the plan that stands: the first call of a target on a plan keeps them under `state` `catalog:<target>:form` (`{epoch, rows}`, ledger op `f-form`), and every later call of that target on the same plan reads them instead of the rows as they stand; side calls and the question for a row read the rows as they stand. A row that moved since is said in the call's projection (`window_est:<call>`, `catalog_moved` 1), and the tap of its answer orders a rebuild of its own when nothing else is due: `curate_mark` `catalog`, a clock order like every other (`./stats` counts it under `rebuild` `by_mark` `catalog`). That rebuild asks no summarizer and no model -- the plan of before with its cover, summary and pins -- but it is more than a new cut: the collection of `gc` runs as in a cold rebuild, with a warm cache too (`gc_on_cold` is not asked), so the window may come out shorter, one cache write per target whose window the collection changes; it never backs off after a failed one. The rows of every target are compared, not only the ones a call reads: a moved row of another target orders the rebuild as well. It takes effect like every rebuild: with `recall_in_wall` "1" a turn that keeps its cut (GH #1471) ends on the plan it began on, and only the first call of a turn orders it. With `recall_in_wall` "0" there is no turn row: every call of a round can order it, and the rebuild can take effect inside a round that runs. The changed prefix after it is the rebuild's own, no prefix break. The budget of a call (`input_soft`) is read off the same kept rows.

**The watch judges the tree a target holds (GH #1497).** `prefix_break` compares the system part of a call with the one the target HOLDS: the system part of the newest earlier call to that target that listed one. A call that sends no system part (an errand that leaves the composite) leaves the tree as it was; compared only when both calls listed one, the call after such a call was never judged, so a leaf that joined the tree there went unseen and only one that left was counted. A leaf that comes and a leaf that goes now both count as a break without a rebuild. The tool menu the target holds is judged the same way, off the `tools` entry of the same call: the provider sends the menu before the system part, so a scope that comes, goes or names other tools without a rebuild is a break as well.

**A round keeps its prefix, and the window watches itself (GH #1463).** A turn is answered in several calls, one per tool round, and each of them must begin with the whole window of the call before: a provider that signs its thinking refuses a replay under another prefix, and a prefix cache pays for every byte that moved. With `recall_in_wall` on, every turn keeps what its first call was cut by ([#1471](https://github.com/mmeyerlein/meclaw/issues/1471)): the parked row `pending:turn_cut:<rk>:<session>:<turn>` holds the text of the plan, the cover the wall started at (also when no plan stood yet), and the two leaves of `history` a rebuild replaces or takes down, the summary leaf and the handover leaf, each as the hash of its block and the audience bound to it. The row also keeps the session legend of the first call (`legend`): how far the wall reached then and, when another session stood in it, each session's span, so the frames `[this session, since …]`, `[earlier session, …]` and `[other session, …]` stay as the first call set them (or stay away, when it set none) for everything it sent; a text of another session that comes while the turn runs is added behind with `[other session, since …]`, and nothing before it moves. Every later call of the turn (a tool iteration, a side call, the final one) is cut by that row and not by the plan that stands; `./intake` reads the row in the leg that reads the plan, so a later call sends no message more to the store. The answer check of `./intake` reads the same row for the window of the call it checks, and the plan that stands once the row is gone. A rebuild no longer waits for anything: it starts when its trigger comes, writes its plan, its summary leaf and its actions when it is done, and shows in the first call of the next turn. Two turns that run at once, in two sessions of one audience, each keep their own row. What an answer adds to the plan (its blocks, and which recall blocks were left out) is written into the turn's row too, so a recall block is still decided once. When a rebuild wrote its plan in the middle of a turn, what that turn decides from there on stays in its row and does not reach the new plan: the next turn checks those recall blocks once more, as a rebuild checks every block anyway. The row goes with the answer that ends the turn (a final answer, an error, a timeout); the answer of a side call ends no turn and leaves the row, and an answer of an older call of the turn that comes home late, while a newer call of it is out, does take it down -- the next call of the turn is then cut like a first call, and a rebuild that came between counts in `prefix_breaks`; a row no answer took down is swept with the parked rows after `pending_sweep_s`, and a call of the turn that comes after that is cut like a first call. The actions a rebuild leaves for the next call (`actions_pending`) are taken by the first call of the next turn. When two calls of one turn are both the first (a side call beside the turn's own), the row is laid insert-if-absent in the bundle of the reads and read back in the same bundle; the call that finds another call's row is read once more, cut by that row: its two reads (the hashes, the window) a second time, two messages to the store. In the regular case that repeat is all a call pays for the row, and only the call that lost pays it. Two more cases pay messages, each once: a call whose laying of the row was put off for `./handover` or a relay and that then takes neither lays the row on one more pass (three messages), and a call sent again from the kept copy of a first call, after the tap of that call set the row forth, is read once more by the row (two messages). For a turn that keeps a row, the figure `prefix_breaks` excuses another state of the plan only between two turns; inside such a turn only the guard at the hard line excuses, so two calls of it cut by two plans would be counted, also when its row was swept. With `recall_in_wall` `0` no turn keeps a row: a rebuild may change the window between two calls of a turn, and the call after it has no verdict, as before. The guard at the hard line stays, it is the one cut a running turn can still get. Across the round boundary the rule is narrower: the next round's first call begins with the window before it up to that window's running round, the place the model's second cache mark stood at. What lay behind it was never in a cached prefix and may move once: the notes block goes to the new words, and the recall block of the round that ended is decided.

With `snapshot_window` on, every call judges its own window against the `sent` list of the call before it to the same target (a bridge is a window of its own and is not compared) and the `curate` mark of the call carries the verdict. `prefix_break` is 1 when the call did not begin as the rule above says: within a round any difference from the whole window before, across the boundary a difference before the running round of the window before, in the system part a different tree. A call after a rebuild or after the guard at the hard line has no verdict, a rebuild is the one place a prefix may change; for a turn that keeps a row (`recall_in_wall` on) a rebuild excuses between two turns only, as said above. Whether a rebuild lay between two calls is told by the state of the plan, never by time: the snapshot row of a call keeps the epoch of the plan it was built on (`plan_epoch` in `cut`), and two calls with different epochs had a rebuild between them, also when the first of them left while the summarizer still worked. `window_lost` counts the elements of the kinds words of a person, answer and failed tool result that the window before sent as they stand in the wall, that lie above the plan's cover (no summary holds them) and that this call sends in no form. An element whose bytes the cut made (the words under the notes block, a one-line form) names no wall row and is not judged; a recall block left out by a decision is none of these kinds. `thinking_dropped` is the number of replayed thinking blocks the provider dropped on the call: the model's cell names them in the body of its answer (`meta.thinking_dropped`), the tap counts them. The figure `window` of the `stats` door sums all three; each should read 0, for every role alike.

**The brief (`summary_form`, GH #1440).** With `summary_form` `brief` a rebuild asks for a handover brief instead of a chronicle: what carries the exchange on, not the order it went in. One line per item, `- <who> (<YYYY-MM-DD>)[ <subject>/<predicate>]: <statement>[ [<stance>]][ [superseded <YYYY-MM-DD>]][ [#<12 hex>]]`; `who` is a speaker -- `person`, `agent:self`, `peer:<ref>`, key required -- or one of the kinds `topic` (what the person is busy with, in the person's words), `promised`, `open`, `dead_end` (a refused suggestion, an unwanted topic, a failed tool way; dated, never a judgement of the person) and `intent` (what the companion meant to do next), whose key may be left out: a keyless `topic` or `intent` takes the kind as its key, a keyless `promised`, `open` or `dead_end` line is added and never replaces one. Every transcript line starts with the short id of its block, and the brief so far goes in at the top as such, not as a summary to retell. The answer is parsed deterministically: an answer without one line of the form is rejected -- the old window and summary stay, the rebuild ends as a failed one, and the mark `summary_rejected` `{kept: 0, dropped}` says it; a mixed answer keeps its lines and says the rest in the same mark. A new line with the speaker and key of an older one takes the older one's place, marked `[superseded <old date>]`; a line said again word for word replaces nothing, a key may be lower case of any script, and any other line is appended -- the speaker is part of the key, so the companion's line never corrects one of the person's. Every line points at a source of its summary: an id that names none (missing, a kept turn, unknown) becomes the newest row that went in. `sources` are wall rows only -- the brief before's and this time's, never a summary's hash. The ids stay in the leaf `history.summary` (the model reads them, `history_read` follows them) and out of every voice and phone seed (`./handover` strips them). A brief that follows a prose summary starts anew. `prose` (shipped) = the chronicle of before, byte-identical.

**The reference target condenses itself (`summarizer_self`, GH #1440).** With `summarizer_self` `1` a rebuild sends no request to `./summarizer`. It sends ONE call to the reference target (the one that holds its tree), after the answer of the turn: the system of the round's last call (every family whole, the gated and the session ones empty included), that call's messages from its `call_blocks` as the prefix, the answer it got, and the instruction last as a user message -- the head that says the answer is never shown, the summary prompt of the `summary_form`, the transcript. Everything but the tail is the prefix the provider already caches, so the role's model condenses at the cached price. The call carries `hop.summarize` `1`, `hop.summary_call` and `hop.summary_token`; its answer comes home on `in_summary_self` and is handled as a summarizer answer, the mark `summary_self` beside the kept summary. When a block of the last call is gone, the rebuild has no call, the rebuild is one for an expired cache (the prefix is cold then) the last call was a tool round (the newest answer belongs to the turn before) or the reference target is not free -- a turn of the session is parked, waiting for its call (GH #1448), `./summarizer` condenses as before. The mark `summary_self` names the call, the model, the reason of the rebuild (`why`) and the call's `tokens_prompt`, `tokens_cached` and `tokens_completion`. The role's cell is busy while it condenses: the person's next turn waits until the call is done, and the default stays off until it is measured. A role that streams its answer (`partial_unit`) sends partials of this call too; no edge takes them, so each one ends in the dead letters as `no_route`. The self call sends the role's cell the last call's whole system, so the policy clears `system_hash_sent` when the call leaves and again when its answer comes home, and the next call sends every family whole. The parent must route the answer home and nowhere else: talky's edges keep it from the splitter, the partials, the errors and every exit. `0` (shipped) = the rebuild of before, byte-identical.

**The role's own lines (GH #1272).** A conversation's window reached the model's soft line practically never (luna 250000), so a role's `tiers`, `keep_rounds` and day horizon never applied and whole days lay raw in the window. Two more marks order a rebuild under soft, package or not: `target` when the window reaches the target's `window_target` (tokens; example sets talky 100000, quick 12000, 0 = off, a profile or the instance sets its own), and `day` for a role with `horizon` `day` when the turn is on another UTC day than the newest round of the round's last rebuild (`state` `last_rebuild_day:<rk>`, written by every rebuild of such a role; a round that never rebuilt starts at the day of its first answer). The turn's day is its own time where `./intake` stamped one (a replay channel's `happened_at`, the clock the rows age by), the tap's clock otherwise. Both aim at `window_target` times `rebuild_to` / `compress_at`, never above a soft rebuild's aim (`day` of a role without a target at the soft aim). A `target` rebuild that leaves the window over the target (the rounds kept raw alone are that large) is not repeated on every turn: the round keeps the window of its last `target` order (`state` `target_at:<rk>`), lowered on every later turn to that turn's window when it is smaller, so the row holds the window the rebuild left; the next `target` waits until the window has grown by `window_target` minus the aim from there, or has fallen under the target; until then the mark is `none` and the turn's `curate` stamp says `target_hold`. The order is `hard`, `soft`, `day`, `target`, `cost`. A `day` rebuild keeps raw only the `keep_recent` rounds of the running day, so the day before takes its tier form (talky: the summary) -- its tool results included -- and today stays raw; outside the raw token budget only: the rounds within `keep_recent_tokens` stay raw through the day boundary (GH #1427). Every turn's `curate` stamp also names the raw part of its call, `raw_tokens` and `raw_rounds` (the wall rows above the plan's cover that the call sends -- a quick call its few -- and their rounds; `raw_tokens` is the raw token budget's own figure of those rows -- stamp and budget count alike, GH #1434 --, while `window_tokens` stays the provider's figure).

**A refused read (GH #1225).** A ledger leg the store refused is never read as an empty answer. A call's window reads once more (the reason carries `reread:`; the normal path takes no extra hop); a second refusal sends the call with its system part and its round, without what is missing, and says so: one `cuts` entry per missing part (`what` `wall`, `slots` or `pins`, `shown` 0, `error_code`, `legs`) and one `store_error` mark. A hash read refused twice (`b-wallh`, `b-keeph`, `b-slotsh`, `b-pins`, the parked turn `b-slimq`, or the cover `b-cover` of a call whose plan holds none) is the same cut of the part it was read for, never a `missing_block` for a block that stands. When `./intake` could not read a turn's plan or cover, the parked turn names those reads (`wall_unread`, `a-plan`/`a-cover`): the call shows its own turn only -- no older row, no `missing_block` -- with one `cuts` entry `{what: wall, error_code: store_error, legs: <the reads>}`; the `store_error` mark is `./intake`'s. With its slots unknown a call sends no system diff and stamps no `system_hash_sent`; a parked turn out of reach twice leaves no call, only the mark and a line on stderr. A rebuild step stops with the mark: a refused probe starts no rebuild, a refused claim is no lost race, a refused question or answer read frees the claim by its token. A refused tap read writes nothing it would derive from the answer's usage, only the round's last call and the mark. Every state row, block and model pin is written in one step (`insert` with `replace` on the key, `if_absent` on the hash, or on hash, source and audience for a pin), so a refused write never loses the row it replaces; a refused window-form write empties the child's form cache, and the next call puts every form again.

## The ways of a call (`path_routes`, GH #1247)

The route cell of the composite stamps every turn (`talk`, `simple`, `complex`, `research`); the collector carries the path on the seam as `hop.path` and the parent's edge lifts it into `context.path`. The stamp recommends, the lead decides (R-WP-1). **D58 target: the curator names no way and no cell.** The lead's `path_routes` names, per path, a target (a model cell the composite wires, `hop.target`) or an errand class of `departures` (the call leaves the composite on `errand`); `*` names the target of every other turn. `fallback` gives a target the one a round falls back to -- a target with one is a fast one --, `spec_target` the target of a speculative call, `bridge_target` the target of a bridge, `errand_tool` the tool a speaking model starts an errand with. They are knobs of the lead ([`lead`](../lead/README.md)) and all ship empty; a composite sets them at its `lead` hive. talky's values, as the text below speaks of them: `path_routes` `talk:brain,simple:quick,complex:consult,research:research,*:brain`, `departures` `consult:10000,research:120000:240000`, `fallback` `quick:brain`, `spec_target` `quick_spec`, `bridge_target` `*:quick,spec:quick_bridge`, `errand_tool` `consult_cogny` (at `./intake` as well), `errand_family` `consult`. Below, "way `quick`" is the fast target, "way `brain`" the target that holds its tree, "`consult`"/"`research`" the errand classes. `quick` is the short window of its target's profile with the tools of the profile's `allow` (`tools` `scoped`) callable and the stamp's other families as hints (R-WM-1: the curator owns what the model sees); `consult` and `research` are one mechanism (R-WP-9): the whole errand leaves for the backend under its own `consult_id`, without a call of the voice model (R-WP-5), and the session's speaking calls carry the pending note (`consult.pending`) until the round that answers it -- recognised by `consult_id` on its context -- leaves on `brain` and takes the note down. The note stands in the notes block that opens the person's newest message, never in the system part (GH #1497): as a leaf it joined the tree after the consult started and left it with the advice, two breaks of the cached prefix without a rebuild; it is shown whatever the role's `notes_share` leaves, and a side call beside such a call keeps it as a leaf of its own system part (a side window has no notes block and is not compared). The state row `consult_pending:<session>` holds the open consult; its `until` is the errand's stamp plus the class's `pending_ms` in `departures`, else `consult_pending_ms` (GH #1260: a `research` result comes in a later turn, after the wait the bridge names). **While it stands, no speaking call can start a second consult (GH #1280).** Every speaking call of the session refuses the errand tool (`errand_tool`, talky: `consult_cogny`) at the call: the call names it in `hop.tools_refused` (a comma list; a scope whose `allow` does not hold the tool has nothing to refuse), the composite lifts the key into the context on every model call edge (`''` when the call names none), and its dispatcher does not send a call of a refused tool and answers it as a tool error (`tool_refused`): no second consult, and the model gets one call more and speaks to the person, so a turn whose only call was refused is never silent until the advice of the consult that runs arrives. The menu stays as it was (GH #1497, review I2 of the strand): until then the tool left the `tool_scope` with a `deny` the moment a consult started and came back with its advice, and as the provider sends the menu before the system part, that broke the cached prefix twice per consult, unseen by the watch. The pending note stays beside it as information: told only in the note not to consult again, the brain of a `talk` turn consulted again anyway, as a tool call without a word beside it (production log of 2026-10-20: a second consult 9 s after the first, about 0.09 $). A consult the brain starts itself sets the same mark: `./intake` takes the ids of the calls of its `errand_tool` in the model's output into the tap (`consults`, the folded form `<family>{op: "<op>"}` of a tool `<family>_<op>` too; empty `errand_tool`: none), and `./policy` writes the mark in its phase `tap` under the consult id the dispatcher stamps -- a follow-up's `consult_id` argument, else the call's id; cogny's advice comes back under it -- only while none stands, so a consult the curator sent keeps its own. With the `consult` family folded (`context.tools_folded`) the menu entry is `consult`, and the refusal names it as well (the dispatcher matches the unfolded name). The advice ends the mark and gives the tool back; an ask back of the backend (`consult_class` `ask`) does not, the consult still runs. `consult_pending_ms` is only the backstop for an advice that never comes.

**Memory turns (`memory_to_brain`, GH #1435).** A question about the person's own people, life or past can be stamped `simple`, and the quick window carries no memory rows -- the quick voice then had nothing to answer from but invention. With the knob on, a turn whose path leaves on `quick` (`path_routes`; shipped: `simple`) and whose stamp names `memory` in `needs` (a set adds the need to `talky/route`) leaves on `brain` instead; the brain call sees the turn's recall rows, the `route` mark says `reason: memory`, and a speculative answer of the turn is dropped (table below). The stamp's field decides; the policy reads only the name. A delta stamp of a memory turn is refired, not reused (below). A turn on another way (shipped: `talk`, `complex`, `research`, unstamped) and a consult's answer change nothing; off (`0`, shipped) the way is the way of before. The collector's `needs_wait_recall` lets such a turn wait for its recall leg.

**Unneeded recall (`recall_by_needs`, GH #1441, GH #1448).** The recall is looked up while the route stamp is still out, so a turn that needs no memory carried its recall result whole. With the knob on, a brain call of a stamped turn whose `needs` do not name `memory` lowers the prio of the running turn's ambient recall block (the collector's pair, `call_recall_` + 16 hex) to `block_prio` `recall_unneeded` in that call's snapshot -- the plan keeps the preset, so a later turn sees `recall` (OR-38-65 b): the result reaches the model whole -- never dropped for the stamp alone; stage 2 cuts it first under budget pressure. The model's own `memory_recall` keeps its prio; a gap find of `./push` is a `push` block. A `talk` turn whose stamp is a fallback (`hop.fallback`: the route did not judge the needs) and a turn without a path keep it too; the collector carries `hop.fallback` only with its knob `needs_no_recall` on, so a set switches both together. Only the running round of a call to the target that holds its tree; a call with the short window of a fast target never carries the recall. Off (`0`, shipped) the plan of before.

**The speaker after a tool result (T4-10, GH #1248).** A quick round that called a tool comes back on the seam with the result (`iter` >= 1), and `./policy` names the speaker again: the quick voice keeps it only while the result is speakable as it is -- every tool call of the turn has its result (no open gap), the newest result is neither empty nor an error object (`error`/`error_code`), and the round is at most `quick_max_iter` iterations past its first call. Anything else leaves on `brain` with ONE `escalate` mark (`{call, path, from: quick, to: brain, reason, iter}`, reason `iterations`, `gap` or `unspeakable`), and the state row `escalated:<session>` keeps the turn with the brain for the rest of its round. The brain's call is the whole window: the quick way cleared `system_hash_sent`, so its tree goes whole.

**The side call (D58 moveout, GH #1476).** The curator knows no bridge any more: it offers a conditional side call. The lead's verdict carries `side`, a list of entries `{when, target, window, text, fill?, first}`; `./policy` sends one call beside the main call for each entry whose condition holds -- `external` when the main call leaves the composite, `must_call` when the cut makes the call its required tool round; every entry whose condition holds goes (which entries to name is the lead's choice); a verdict that names none sends no bridge (OR-d58-2.target.71): an entry without a `target` stays in, said on stderr, like one without a slot. Every side call is built the same: the target's leaves with the frame slot, without the sidecar contract, the gated and session families whole, the entry's `text` as the leaf of the target's side slot (its profile's `instructions.side_slot`, talky `bridge`: the leaf `instructions.bridge`; a target that names none or has reported no profile yet takes the entry's `slot`, which the lead fills from its knob `side_slot`, shipped `bridge`; neither: no side call, said on stderr), `tool_scope` `{allow: []}`, the messages of its `window` (`turn`: the person's turn alone; `wall`: the target's short window), its own `calls` row (`trigger` `side`), its own snapshot and its own budget from the target's catalogue row; its head carries `call_kind` `side`, and its answer never ends the turn. **The errand's mark (GH #1248).** An errand that leaves the composite carries its wait as `hop.consult_wait_ms` -- read by no cell, a mark for whoever reads the message log -- and the lead's row `consult_pending:<session>` keeps it as `wait_ms`. **A tool round without its call goes to the brain (GH #1260, OR-WG-9).** The required call carries `hop.interim` `tool`. **A recall the turn surely does not need (`recall_skip_sure`, GH #1466).** `recall_by_needs` only lowers a prio: the recall of a turn that needs no memory still stands in the window whole. With `recall_skip_sure` on, the recall pair the collector fetched for the running turn is left out of the window when the turn's route stamp surely says the turn needs no memory. Sure means all of: the stamp is judged (the seam carries a path and no `hop.fallback`), `memory` is not among its `needs`, and the yes-value of the memory question -- `hop.needs_yes`, a JSON object string the collector puts on the seam when the route cell stamps `needs_yes` (its knob `stamp_needs_yes`) -- is a number from 0 to 1 and at most `recall_skip_max_yes` (inclusive). The certainty lies on the answer to the memory question, not on the stamp's `p`, which is the confidence of the path. The rule decides once, when the pair arrives and before it was ever sent: the pair goes whole, with its `tool_call`, before the plan is made, so it counts neither in the window nor in the estimate. The plan keeps the decision under `left_out`, and no later call and no rebuild takes the block up again. Whatever is unsure keeps the block: no stamp, a stamp that came after the pair already left with a call of its round, a fallback stamp, a stamp without `needs_yes` (the route cell's knob is off, or another classifier), a yes-value that is no number from 0 to 1 (a bool is none), a yes-value over the bound, a bound that is no number from 0 to 1. A `memory_recall` the model called itself is never touched, and a pair whose content already stands in the plan stays. The rule asks for no way and no role: it holds for every call that carries a stamp. What it leaves out is said three times: on the hop as a cut `recall_skip_sure` (one entry per block), as one mark `recall_skipped` per block (canonical JSON `{block, call, yes}`: the block id as in the snapshot, the call, the yes-value read) so a watcher can count the share, and in the snapshot (`cut.dropped`, and with `snapshot_window` on in `sent`: one entry per element of the pair, `form` `cut`, `why` `recall_skip_sure`). The lookup itself still runs, at once and beside the classifier: the rule saves window, not the lookup, and adds no wait. A set switches three values together: the route cell's `stamp_needs_yes`, `recall_skip_sure` and `recall_skip_max_yes`; with `recall_skip_sure` on and `stamp_needs_yes` off every block stays. Off (`0`, shipped) the window and the plan of before, byte for byte.

**Notes (`in_note`, GH #1303).** A pin stands in the system part, changes the cached head and lives until its `until`; a candidate is shown once, on a trigger, as a recall pair. Order: `priority`, then `turn` before `session` before `until`. Over `notes_share` of the usable window the last of that order goes first (a window not known yet shows every note; a share of 0 none). A `turn` note belongs to the turn whose window first saw it (`used_turn`, shown or left out), a `session` note ends with the close of its session (`in_close`, the session it came in, or any session of its round when it named none), an `until` note at its time or its withdrawal. A ledger without the `notes` table holds none.

**A note shown once (`once`, D58 note, GH #1477).** With `note_once` "1" on `./intake`, `./policy` and `./history` a note may carry the scope `once`: it stands in the calls of exactly one turn -- like a `turn` note, the first window that shows it and every later call of that turn, the same block -- or in the start window of exactly one running session with its renewals, and then nowhere. It never rides a live feed: `./history` reads it for the next `seed` through a reader of its own beside the pinned `seed_notes`, in the one `Notes:` line and under `seed_entries_max`, and books it in the step of that `seed`; a renewal of the same call carries it on. **State line (`state_block`, GH #1439).** With `state_block` "1" the window of a speaking way opens the notes block with one line of what is going on, built from the ledger without a model: `[state -- today: <topic>; promised: <a> | <b>; open: <x>; set today: <y>]`. `today` is the newest named topic of the round -- with `topic_aging` "1" aged as the live feed ages it (`topic_aged`, GH #1436: none in a session that did not name it, none after `topic_age_turns` turns without a renewed mention) --, `promised` the first line of each open pin of source `commitment`, `open` one `a delegation` per open `pending:delegation:<session>#*` row whose `until` lies ahead, `a consult` while `consult_pending:<session>` stands, and `gap: <words>` per gap of today without an addendum, `set today` the `fact` marks of today (UTC), oldest first, a fact whose source is not the person's with ` (<source>)`, one not plainly asserted with ` [<stance>]`. **The session boundary (GH #1335).** The window reads the wall over every session (OR-KX-K4), so once another session stands in it, every user text of a session whose newest row lies before the start of the running one opens with `[earlier session, since <start> -- from before this session, not said now]`, every user text of a session that overlaps it (another channel's open session) with `[other session, since <start>]`, and the first user text of the running session with `[this session, since <start>]`; `<start>` is the earliest row the wall still holds of that session (after a rebuild covered its opening, the first row after the cover; the running session's start moves the same way, so an overlapping session can then read as earlier). **The person's frame (GH #1337).** Every block the window puts in front of the person's words in their element -- the notes block, a recall or brief folded into the turn that opens the window -- carries a frame, so the words get one too: whenever anything but their own session boundary stands before them, the person's words open with `[the person, now -- their own words to you]` on a line of its own, between their short id (with short ids on) and the words; in an element of an earlier round -- a turn of the past that opened the window with a folded recall -- the frame is `[the person -- their own words]`, never "now". Facts, never an instruction; the facts themselves reach the voice through the next seed, which carries every one of them (the notes as a `Notes:` line, an inner turn as a `(<kind>) <text>` line) and writes `seed_facts:<live>` itself. Nothing moved, nothing is sent; a turn without a handle has no row and sends nothing; a handle without a seed or feed compares against nothing.

**When the answer comes (#1400, section `horizon`; D58 moveout, GH #1476).** The waiting-time advise is the lead's ([`lead`](../lead/README.md), knob `horizon_advise`): the curator knows no horizon any more. It offers the conditional feed: the lead's verdict carries `feed`, a list of entries `{when, section, body, once?, mark?}` under the same two conditions as a side call (`external`, `must_call`). For each entry whose condition holds `./policy` sends the body unchanged as ONE `live_feed` -- the hop `section`, `live`, `session_id`, `turn_id` and the round, no `hop.at_turn_end` -- WITH the round's sends, over the one road of the feeds (`own_feed`, "A feed on order" below): its mark `feed` carries the entry's `mark`, and it rides with the sends -- stored with them for an awaited spec answer or a delta, sent with them, gone when the round never goes (the mark is then a held write, `horizon_ops`, written only when the sends go); an entry with `once` takes its claim's round trip first (`feed-send`), that step riding with the sends the same way. And `./policy` tells the lead of every round a condition held for -- `lead_note` `round` with `when`, `call` and the time of the seam `at_ms` --, also when no feed goes, so a family without a default learns its first duration; held, the note rides with the sends and counts only when they go. The answer that closes the round (a final answer, not a side call, no tool call, no error, no cut, not the tool round itself) adds `round_end` (ms) to the note `turn_end` it sends anyway. The lead books the durations in its own store; the ledger holds no `horizon_rounds`, no `pending:horizon:` row and no `horizon_sent` or `horizon_hit` mark any more.

**A feed on order (D58 feed, GH #1481, `in_feed`).** A domain orders that something goes into a running session now, without waiting for a turn's end; `./policy` sends it at once, with no curate and no model call, as ONE `live_feed`: the head as ordered without `route` (`live`, `session_id`, `turn_id`, `section`, `sender`, `at_turn_end` and every other field; the round `audience_set` from the head, else from the context, in its canonical form), the body as ordered without its field `mark`. `at_turn_end` is never read: the receiver waits at its own turn boundary. Every sent feed books ONE mark `feed {section, sender, once?, mark?}` in the order's session, turn and round -- `mark` the orderer's field, uninterpreted -- written in the same step as the send. With `hop.once` (a key of the orderer, without `#`) the order claims `pending:feed:<live>#<turn>#<once>` first (`if_absent`, `{parked_at}` for the A13 sweep) and goes only where the claim took, after exactly one round trip to the store; a claim another chain holds sends nothing and books nothing. Booked instead of sent, with `reason` in the mark: no `live` in the head (`no_live` -- the policy keeps no state "a session runs"), a `once` with `#` (`bad_once`: it would part the claim's key wrong), the id of a `once` note (`once_note`: a `once` note never rides a feed), and a note whose read the store refused (`store_error`: whether it is a `once` note is unknown, and unknown is never sent). An order whose body names a note (`source`, `id`) reads that note's rows and the held state of the start window `seed_facts:<live>` in the same round trip as its claim; when the note is one a seed of the handle carries (a live session or until note) and the state is held without it, its id `<source>:<id>` is entered in the same step as the send -- by compare-and-set on the value read, its digest computed by the rule of `seed_fact_state`, because `./history` writes the same key with every start window: a lost write leaves history's state standing. So the next renewal neither names the fed note again nor renews for it alone; any other order leaves the state untouched. The curator's own feeds take this road whatever `feed_in` says, so they are booked the same way and leave as before (no `sender`, no `once` on the hop): the tool fact (`once` `tool_fact`, `mark: {tool}`) and the renewal (no claim, so its moment stays: its mark rides the step that writes the held state, `section` `renew`). A domain beside the curator orders on the lane like any other -- the lead of a composite orders the early interrupt there (D58 moveout, below).

**A crisis heard on a partial (#1404; D58 moveout, GH #1476).** The early interrupt is the lead's ([`lead`](../lead/README.md), knobs `interrupt_on_delta`, `interrupt_questions`): the curator knows no crisis value, no question and no interrupt. `./policy` hands every delta stamp (`in_delta_stamp`) to the lead on `lead_spec` with the round, the handle and the session, and the lead ORDERS the interrupt like any domain, on `in_feed` with `once` -- the one road above sends ONE `live_feed` per turn (claim `pending:feed:<live>#<turn>#interrupt`), books its mark `feed` with the lead as `sender` and `mark {delta_n}`, and books an order without a handle as not sent (`no_live`). The composite draws `./lead -> ./curator` on `in_feed` and sets `feed_in` "1". What the curator booked before the move -- `interrupt_skipped` for a fallback stamp, `interrupt_early {delta_n, ms_before_turn_end}` at the turn's end -- the lead keeps in its own store.

**The turn path waits for its delegation (#1407).** On the phone the live model delegated the person's turn, and beside the delegation round the turn's own path went on: a second tool call, then the quick voice's second round ran 20 s into its timeout. A delegation round (`./assemble` keys it `<turn>~<deadline_ms>~<hex8>`, `delegation_id` beside it) puts `pending:delegation:<session>#<turn>` at its first curate (`delegation_id`, the round, `until` = the key's deadline, `parked_at` for the sweep). A curate of a running session's own turn path while that row stands and `until` is ahead sends no call: its parked turn goes and the mark `turn_parked {delegation_id, iter}` is booked; the delegation answers the turn. A late delegation, one of another turn, an advice round and a turn without a handle change nothing; a turn with a handle reads the one row more. The key's scope is the running session's handle `live`, else the session: the delegation round runs under a session of its own, the turn path under its own, and both carry the handle; the park always carries the round's `audience_set`, and the rebuild (which knows no handle) reads every delegation row and keeps the ones whose audience the round may see -- one colony, one member (D38 V3, GH #1451; D58 live: the knob `delegation_park_by_call` fell, every composition parks so).

**An errand without an answer leaves its outcome (`errand_outcome`, GH #1469).** A delegation round can end without an answer: its call comes back with `finish_reason` "error", or nothing comes back before the deadline in its turn key. Before, the ledger then held the `(delegation)` row alone; a rebuild counted it as the scaffold of a closed errand and shrank it, and on the next turn the model knew neither of the failure nor of the deadline, though the person had heard the promise. With `errand_outcome` "1" `./policy` keeps both signals at the parked row of the errand (`pending:delegation:<scope>#<turn>`): the tap of the round adds `ended` "failed" and the `error_code` when it is in the form of a code (`[a-z][a-z_]{0,31}`; the text of a provider never enters), or `answered` 1 when a final answer of the round reached the wall. The first curate of the session after such an end -- failed, or `until` behind the clock without `answered` -- files one wall row of kind `outcome` under the turn of the errand: `<first line of the (delegation) row> -- outcome of this errand: failed (<code>)`, or `... no answer`. The row is numbered behind every row the call read, so it stands at the end of the wall and in the window of that very call; `./policy` writes it itself, the one kind of wall row `./intake` does not file. The mark `errand_outcome` `{delegation_id, outcome}` is written once per errand, and the parked row carries `outcome` from then on; block, wall row and mark are inserts guarded by their own keys, so two curates in flight file one row. A curate of the errand's own round files nothing: an escalation after a failed call runs under the same turn, and its answer or its next failure decides. The sweep of parked rows (`pending_sweep_s`) leaves a delegation row that has neither `answered` nor `outcome` while its `until` is ahead -- the errand may still be answered. Past `until` the sweep takes the row and files its outcome in the same go, in the session the park names (`errand_session`, with the round `errand_round`): a session that never has another turn, or a running session that has ended (its park keyed by the handle), gets its outcome too, and no such row stays in the sweep's read for good. Once a curate has filed the outcome, the next sweep takes the row as any other. The row stays like a tool result that failed: no plan shrinks it, the class `scaffold` never counts it, `window_lost` watches it (block kind `outcome`), and stage 2 cuts it at the prio `block_prio.outcome`, or at that of `tool_result` while the table does not name the kind. The transcript a summary or a session note is made from reads it as a note, never as the person's words. A late answer arrives as before and stands behind the row. Nothing waits and nothing polls: the check runs on the next curate of the session, or on the next sweep. One limit: the park holds one row per turn of the person, and with "1" it is written only while none stands, so of two errands handed on in one turn only the first is followed. The second never parks over it: its own round gets the mark `errand_outcome` `{delegation_id, outcome: "not followed", followed: <the first's id>}`, once, nothing files an outcome for it, and the turn's own path parks until the first's `until` only; a round delivered again finds its own row and writes no mark. Several parks per turn (one row per errand) are not built. What the person hears at the moment an errand fails is no part of this. "0" (shipped) reads, writes and sends nothing more: the park is the unconditional set of before, and a second errand of the turn parks over the first without a mark.

**A tool that failed is never spoken by the quick voice (#1279).** Whether a tool result failed is read off its structure, never its words: the collector parks a result whose header carried an `error_code` with that code on its parked row (never on the turn, which has no key for it) and names it in the `tool_errors` slot of the iteration's curate (`{tool_call_id: error_code}`, this iteration's results only); `./intake` carries the slot into the parked turn, where `./policy` reads it, and since GH #1468 judges every tool result once when it files it -- failed by the slot or by its text -- and writes the verdict beside the wall row (`wall.failed`), for the rebuild. A result so named, or one whose text is an error object, is a failed tool -- a failure told in prose alone included. After such a result the quick voice gives the round to the brain (`escalate`, reason `unspeakable`), and the brain's window carries ONE turn note `tool_error` (source `curator`, priority 0): "tool not reachable" -- the raw error is never the note.

**Push candidates on a quick way (T4-08).** A candidate came into the window on the ask path of `./push` only, ahead of the round because the round waited for the memory. A routed round (`hop.path`, first assembly) does not wait for it, so `./intake` asks in its first read whether a live candidate stands; if one does and nothing of the turn is in the wall yet, the round stays parked, `./intake` hands `./push` a `push_round` (the round's words, `resume` = its parked key), and `./push` picks with its own rules and answers on `in_addendum` with `resume` -- always, an empty pair included -- without a question to the memory. `./intake` writes the pair and reads the round back behind it, so the pair opens the round. The quick window keeps a candidate pair (`call_candidates_*`) although it drops the memory's rows.

**The window rule (R-IG-1, GH #1085, since 1.11.2).** This hive is the one place that cuts what its model reads, and it cuts by shares of the usable window -- the catalog's `input_soft` the llm cell stamps on every answer, cut to the model's own window alone and kept in state `input_soft` -- turned into characters at 3 per token, never by a typed number: an earlier answer's sidecar block and the collector's sidecar contract (`instructions.sidecar`) 10 %, the summarizer's transcript and the handover note's transcript 50 % of the window of the model that reads them, the summarizer's (state `summarizer_input_soft`: the stamp on each of its answers, the bound of a refusal over its window -- a refused note is measured anew once --, before either `input_soft_fallback`), never the served one, the handover block 50 % (and always under the leaf bound of the llm cell's system gate: without a window the 4 MiB carrier ceiling, with one what the window takes at all), the push candidates' part 5 %, a `history_read` 10 % (a larger block by its id is read in pages, a larger turn range refused with `too_large` and its size), a page of `./reader` 10 % of the asker's window. Without a known window everything goes whole; a find said aloud (`fact`) always does, because the voice model that hears it is not the model this hive serves. The summary has no length of its own: the summarizer is asked to keep it as short as useful and as long as needed, and only a summary whose leaf would not fit the carrier (4 MiB, `CARRIER_MAX_BYTES`) is refused (`too_long: <n> > 4194304 bytes`). Over its share a text carries `...[cut: <shown> of <total> chars shown; <hint>]`, a section or block left out `...[dropped: <name> (<n> chars) over budget]` where it stood -- optional contract sections fall from the back, a required one never, and the contract's frame is written anew over the sections kept, so it never asks for a dropped one -- and every cut is also on the message's hop as `cuts` (`{what, shown, total, unit}`). A shortened block names its whole length (`[#<id>] <first line> [shortened, <shown> of <total> chars — history_read("#<id>")]`). Every ask of a producer (`recall`) carries `input_soft` (tokens, absent when unknown) beside `recall_input_soft`; the parent's edges lift only `recall_input_soft` into the context, so the alias is what the memory hive reads. A pin is refused only over the carrier, one system leaf of at most 4 MiB (`too_long: <n> > 4194304 bytes`); a smaller window's own bound refuses it at the llm cell, with its number. The candidates are switched by `push` `candidate_push` ("1", "0" off).

Numeric knobs take numeric strings; `null` or empty means the default. The word-for-word check (GH #1079) has no knob here: its rule rides on the section's hop, set by the parent's splitter knob `sidecar_verify` (`templates/talky/README.md`), and applies only to a section registered with a `lane` -- one registered without a lane books its `mark` and is not checked (GH #1473).

**A quote at its source (GH #1225).** Beside `turn`, `turn+recall` and `answer`, a field's rule may be `source` (`{"memory":{"items":"facts","quote":"source"}}`; the splitter hands the answer along as `verify_answer` for every rule that names `source`): the item names where its words come from -- `source` `person`, `peer:<ref>` (a bare `<ref>` too) or `agent:<name>`, trimmed and case-folded -- and the quote counts only in the rows of exactly that source: `person` the `user` rows of the turn and of the person's episode the turn answers (`episode_turn_id`: a round of advice or of a timer answers the person's row of its turn); `peer:<ref>` the `peer` rows of that speaker (`speaker_ref`) in the same two places; `agent:cogny` the turn's advice rows, `agent:voice` its delegations, `agent:app:<id>` and `agent:timer:<id>` its rows framed `[app <id>]` / `[timer <id>]` (the frame counts only with an `<id>` in the form of a name, `a-z0-9_.-`, at most 64 characters -- a text that merely starts with `[timer ]` stays the person's; the frame is the sender's, no shipped template sets one yet); `agent:self` the answer (`verify_answer`) and the final answers of the episode. So a round without a person row of its own -- the reasoning core's advice, a delegation, a round an app or a timer opened -- still checks a quote of the person. An item without a source is refused as `source_missing`, one in a form the check does not know as `source_unknown`, a quote not in the rows of exactly its source as `quote_not_in_source`, a failed read of those rows as `store_error`; every refusal leaves a `verify_reject` mark beside its `reject`, and a `topic` mark carries the topic's source. An item is never re-filed under another source. A round an app or a timer opens enters as a user turn whose text starts with its frame (`[app <id>] ...`, `[timer <id>] ...`, id lower-case letters, digits and `_.-`, at most 64); its wall row is kind `app`/`timer`, never `user`, so it never becomes the person's episode.

## The lead (`templates/lead`, GH #1474)

The curator holds no choice of a model. A composite that chooses between targets wires a `lead` hive beside it ([`lead`](../lead/README.md): the switch, its store, its knobs) and sets `./policy` `lead_wired` "1"; the curator then asks it across its own boundary: `lead_ask`, `lead_spec` and `lead_note` leave on the rim edge `./policy -> .` (the station keys deleted as on every rim edge), and the verdict comes home on `in_lead` (`. -> ./policy`). The composite draws `./curator -> ./lead` for the three lanes and `./lead -> ./curator` setting `in_lead` (talky). A composite without a lead (cogny, simple-agent) names its target at the curate (`hop.target`).

**Indexes (GH #1225).** Beside `state_key` and `candidates_key` (unique) the ledger declares `slots_path` (unique, above), `wall_episode` (`session_id`, `audience_set`, `episode_idx`, unique) and `pins_round` (`hash`, `source`, `audience_set`, unique) and, not unique, the columns its readers filter and order by: `wall_seq`, `wall_session_turn` (`session_id`, `turn_id`), `wall_session_kind`, `wall_hash`, `blocks_hash`, `marks_kind_seq`, `marks_session_turn`, `calls_call_id`, `summaries_covers`. A store builds them at wake and never drops one; a unique index over rows that collide is not built (one log line) until the copies have healed -- a write of a path replaces all of its rows, `./intake` clears the later copies of an episode index and of a pin once per ledger, across every session (`episode_healed`, stamped in `state` `heal:gh1225`; a child reads the stamp alone first, the pins and the wall only while it is missing), and a pin is written only where none stands -- and the next wake builds it. Existing colonies keep their ledger until re-provisioned: `params` reach a running store only through a new instance.

**The round of a pin (GH #972).** `context.audience_set` of an `in_pin` message is the ceiling: for an app it is its member's round, stamped by the builder's pin edge after every edge of the app. A pin without its own `audience_set` takes the ceiling. A pin that names one keeps it when it is (a) the round of the turn this message belongs to -- `context.turn_round`, stamped once by the edge that raised the turn and never by a config's own edge (the mutation door and the boot pass refuse that as `edge_schema`), so no app can claim it -- or (b) inside the ceiling (narrower, or the ceiling itself). Anything else is not placed and not lost: a stderr line `pin:held source=<s> hash=<12 hex>: <reason>`, and the source pins it again in a turn of that round, whose own stamp is the proof -- nothing is placed out of the ledger. Fail-closed: a chain no turn raised (an app's own timer) has no `turn_round`, so only rounds inside the ceiling are kept; a group goal therefore shows in its group from the next turn of that group on. Since a pin carries its round, every audience group of one source travels in ONE message with ONE `replace_sources`: the replacement ends every live pin of the source in any round except the ones the message carries -- a held pin included, so a turn of another round does not end the group's pin. Without a held pin the replacement is one bundle without a read, and of two replacements in flight the one sent last stands whole. With a held pin `./intake` reads the live set first (`a-live`) and ends only what that read names: two replacements in flight can overtake each other, so a pin of the earlier set that the later one does not carry may stay live until the next replacement; a refused `a-live` ends nothing (stderr) -- the new set stands, the old one ends with the next replacement. Two pins of one text in two rounds are one block and one row (the hash names the text): within one message the first is kept, across messages the later round wins.

**Who a row reaches (GH #925).** Every row of `wall`, `calls`, `marks`, `summaries` and `pins` carries `audience_set`, the canonical JSON array of the round that caused it (`context.audience_set`; a summary and a handover leaf the intersection of their sources and of the round they were made for -- a wider round that joins later does not see them, so whether they appear never hangs on a row it may not see --, a handover note the intersection of every row of its session, so a long session's note may come out narrower than its transcript, never wider; `*` neutral), or `[]` when that round declared none (PP-BD-12, since 1.4.0; NULL only in rows from before the rule, and those reach nobody). A reader -- the window, the handover, the history tools, the recall question, the close batch -- hands a row to a round only when the row's set names `*` or holds the round as a subset (the affinity rule); a NULL row reaches nobody. A round that declares no audience sees the rows of its own session that declare none either, or name `*` -- never a row a declared round said there -- and nothing else, and its call is marked `missing_audience` (a round-less recall question likewise takes no find of a declared gap); each round's clock order carries that round, so a rebuild summarises only what that round may see; a rebuild without a round keeps its plan without a summary (the window shortens, nothing is condensed). A model pin carries the intersection of its `pin` mark and every wall row of its block, NULL when any is. The close batch (`write`) is gated by the close round, `context.audience_set` of `in_close` (the round the generation was opened in): it carries only the rows whose audience holds it, counted in `turn_count`/`round_count`, and a close without a round leaves empty.

## The history tools (`./history`, GH #893)

The model reads its own wall, deterministically and out of this hive's ledger alone: no model, no embedding, and never another model's wall. The schemas are the hive's menu answer (`./schemas` `CURATOR_OFFER`); the answer is a JSON object in the text of one `tool_result`.

| Tool | Arguments | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| `history_search` | `query`; `mode` `phrase` (default: the words in order, any case, any spacing), `exact` (case-sensitive) or `regex` (at most 200 characters); `since`/`until` (ISO-8601, a bare `until` date is the whole day; the time a turn was said, `turn_at` where a replay stamped it, else `at`, GH #1225); `kinds[]`; `limit` 1-20 (8); `context` 0-2 (0) | `hits[]` newest first: `id` (`#<12 hex>`), `seq`, `session_id`, `turn_id`, `at`, `kind`, `excerpt` (120 characters either side of the match), and with `context` the neighbours of the same session as `before`/`after`; `total_hits` (of the rows scanned), `scanned` (rows the round may see), `stopped_at_limit` (the page that filled `limit` was the last one read), `truncated_scan` and `cut_by` (`scan_budget` or `time_budget`) |
| `history_read` | `id` (`#<12 hex>`, bracketed, bare or the full hash), with `offset` (a character of the block's text, 0) and `length` (characters, as many as the budget takes) for a page, **or** `from_turn`/`to_turn` | `blocks[]` in wall order, each the stored element whole (`block`) with its row and `released`; `size`; for a range `skipped_history`. A block by its id over the budget is read in pages (GH #1225): `block` is the element with one page of its text, cut with `...[cut: <n> of <total> chars shown, from offset <o>; the next page: history_read {"id": "#<id>", "offset": <next>}]`, the page's stored form within the budget; beside it `offset`, `length`, `total_chars`, `next_offset` (null on the last page) and `budget` -- the pages together are the block |
| `history_outline` | `since` (the time a turn was said, as in a search) | `sessions[]` oldest first: `first_turn`, `last_turn`, `first_at`, `last_at`, `turns`, and `topics[]` (`name`, `from_turn`, `to_turn`, `open`) out of the `topic` marks (OR-KY-71: the open topic is the name of the newest mark with one and no later `end`, a nameless mark belongs to it; `from_turn` null = begun earlier); the newest 100 sessions of the newest `scan_budget` rows the round may see, `omitted_sessions` the rest, `truncated_scan` |

A refusal is an answer under the same call id, its code in the text (`error`) and in `hop.error_code`: `bad_arguments`, `bad_pattern`, `bad_range` (`since` after `until`, a turn range that runs backwards, an `offset` past the end of the block's text), `not_found` (an id or a turn this wall never carried), `ambiguous` (an id prefix that names two blocks; `candidates` lists them), `too_large` (`size`, `budget`; a block by its id only where paging cannot reach it -- not one character of it fits the budget, or it has no text; a range with more rows the round may see than `scan_budget` `block_count_at_least`, one whose raw text is over ten budgets `raw_size`, both refused before a body is read), `unknown_tool`, `ledger_error`, `missing_audience` (the call's context declares no `audience_set`; nothing is read). The model's own `history_*` calls and their answers are on the wall like every round, but no hits, no neighbours and no blocks of a range; by its id each is read whole. A read of a block TRIM released writes one `marks` row `reread` (the block's hash, the reading turn), once per block and turn; released is what the newest `scan_budget` `release` and `pin` marks of the block the round may see leave in `seq` order (GH #931).

**The row bound (GH #925, OR-BD-86).** Rows the round may not see count toward no budget, no page and no neighbour window, but every read stops at twice `scan_budget` rows in all. Where that leaves the answer open -- the visible rows it reached do not settle what the call answers on the same wall without the others -- the call answers in the cut form and shows nothing it read: a search `hits` [], `truncated_scan` true, `stopped_at_limit` false, `cut_by` `scan_budget` and a `detail`, no `scanned` and no `total_hits`; a `history_read` -- a turn range, or a read whose blocks' `release`/`pin` marks reach the bound -- `too_large` with a `detail` and `budget`, no `block_count_at_least`; an outline `sessions` [], `truncated_scan` true and a `detail`. The form is the same on every wall that reaches the bound, so hidden rows move one bit per call -- the bound was reached -- and never a count. It takes at least `scan_budget` hidden rows among the first twice `scan_budget` + 1 rows one read meets (of the `release`/`pin` marks of a read's blocks, more than `scan_budget` among their newest twice `scan_budget`); for the neighbours of a search with `context` (a side whose `context` + 4 nearest rows hold a hidden one is read again, one ledger round trip, the short sides sharing twice `scan_budget` rows) at least `scan_budget` / hits - `context` - 3 hidden rows of the hit's session on one side -- at the defaults with `context` 2: 245 with 20 hits, 620 with 8. Below that no answer shows a hidden row, its id or a word of it, and every number an answer carries counts rows the round may see, never all rows (`scanned`, `total_hits`, `block_count_at_least`, `size`, `turns`, `omitted_sessions`). Metadata channels (GH #932): since 1.4.0 every reader asks the ledger for the rows the round may see (`where audience_set covers <round>`; a round-less read asks for `[]` and `["*"]` rows of its own session), so a hidden row is never read -- page ends, `scanned`, the push and renewal windows and the per-turn `turn_index` hang on visible rows alone, and the row bound and its cut form are a second line that no longer fires on hidden rows. What stays: how long a read takes (the store still walks the hidden rows), the freshness check of a new session's handover, and the counts of `./stats` (OR-BD-6); where a rebuild cuts the window is no longer among them, the plan being one per round since GH #943 (a `release`/`pin` mark acts only on the windows of rounds that may see it and survives every rebuild of another round). `hop.turn_id` of `turn_write` is `<session_id>#<tag>-<index>`, `tag` the first 8 hex of the SHA-256 of the canonical round, the index counted per session and round. The round pays with that call's answer: a narrower `since`/`until` brings it back (a search also a smaller `limit` or no `context`, a range fewer turns, an outline a later `since`).

**The view (`in_view_ask`, D58 GH #1480; GH #1304 before it).** A view is the curator's short way to a window for a named target: only read, and handed back to whoever asked -- any composition, not one role. The judge of a composition (the route stamp's `decide`, the screen's form) is a model like any other and orders its context this way. `./history` answers out of the wall with exactly one `view`, `{messages, system, truncated}`. `messages` are the earlier turns in the universal form `{origin, type: "text", text}`, oldest first: of the running generation (`context.session_id`; a partial, `context.delta` "1", of any session of the round, live-2 K3) and of the rows the round may see (the gate of `history_read`), never the running turn (`context.turn_id` -- whether it already stands on the wall is a race), the newest `turns` turns. With a target both sides speak (`user`, and `assistant` without its sidecar block, one message per turn and side); without one the person's words alone (several messages of one turn joined by a line break), as the former `turns_ask` gave them. The texts fit `budget_tokens` by the templates' one token estimate (the block `token-estimate v1`); what does not fit loses its oldest turns first and `truncated` is true. The budget is at most 4000 tokens, and 1500 at or below 0 or without one. With a target `system` is a tree of leaves `{text}`: `frame`, the moment as facts, one per line (`now: <ISO with seconds and offset>`, `weekday: <day>`, `timezone: <zone>`, `place: <place>` -- the clock `now_utc` in `timezone`, the knob `place`; the asker renders it its own way); `topic`, the newest named topic, aged with `topic_aging` (against the opening of the running session for a partial), only when there is one; and `system[<target>]`, the family of the target, the contributions of the domains as `{<source>: leaf}`, only when it holds any (a contribution with a subject only for a round that may see it). The asker names target, turns and budget; there is no built-in value per target. The way only reads: no `./intake`, no lead, no plan, no call, no pending claim, no window bound, at most two ledger rounds (rows, topic and contributions in the first, blocks in the second) -- 6 messages, what the former `turns_ask` cost. A question without `context.audience_set` reads nothing and is answered `messages: []` with `hop.refused` `no_round`; `turns` below 1 without a target, or a target named `frame` or `topic` (the view's own leaves, which its family would replace), `bad_arguments`; a ledger read the store refused `messages: []` with `hop.degraded` `store` -- never silence.

**The read context (#1279; D58 GH #1480 made it neutral).** One structure out of the wall, built when it is asked for -- so it is current after every turn the wall holds, without a timer -- by the readers a view and a seed share: `context = {turns: [{turn_id, role, text}], topic, parts, now: {iso, weekday, timezone}, place}`. `turns` are both roles (`user`, and `assistant` without its sidecar block), one entry per turn and role, oldest first; `topic` the name of the newest named `topic` mark ("" when it ended the topic or there is none); `parts` the contributions to the target read, `{<source>: leaf}`; `now` the clock (`now_utc`) in `timezone`; `place` the knob. A view builds its `system` tree out of it (above); the seed answers it under `context` (formerly `judge`). **Contributions (`in_part`, a curate's `parts`; D58 GH #1480).** What a model's window needs beside the conversation belongs to the domain that owns it, not to the curator. A domain puts it down as a contribution `{target, source, value, subject?, until?}` -- on `in_part` (the presenter's screen topics, without a round) or as one entry of `parts` in the body of a `curate`, so it costs no message of its own (the collector's, with `brief_hints` `"1"` and a `part_target`: affinity's answer for the round's one person, [#1279](https://github.com/mmeyerlein/meclaw/issues/1279), about that person). `./history` keeps the value whole and unread and hands it to every view of the target as one leaf under the source's name (the lane table above); `./intake` writes the `parts` of a curate in the curate's own step-A bundle, only at iteration `0`, and one about a `subject` only when the round's one person (the entry of `audience_set` that is no `agent:`) is that subject -- so the stamp of that person's next turn reads how they meant earlier requests, and another person's round reads nothing of it. A seed and the live feed read the family of `seed_target` without the contributions about a person. `in_seed_ask` answers one `seed`: a call that asks for its seed often has no generation yet, so the seed reads the newest rows of the ROUND (the gate of `history_read`, any session, at most 400 rows, a full read leaving its oldest turn out) and the newest named topic of the round. `messages` is one `developer` line of facts (`Facts: now ...; place: ...; topic: ...; <source>: <name>, ...`, only what is there, never an instruction; one part per source of a contribution to the running session's target, the knob `seed_target` ("" = none), naming the `name` of each entry of a structured list, else the contribution's text -- the voice cell takes `developer`, `user` and `assistant`), a `developer` line `Notes: <text>; <text>` of the session and until notes the round sees (oldest first; none, no line), the turns as `user`/`assistant` and the round's inner turns (`advice`, `delegation`, `event`) as `developer` lines `(<kind>) <text>` in their place -- `context.turns` keeps the two roles only --, and the held start window lines (`in_seed_line`) as `developer` messages in the place their sender named -- `head` lines right after the facts line, `tail` lines as the LAST messages, each part by `order`, the anchor among the tail lines by `seed_anchor_order` (today's senders: the wind-down at order 2 after a hint at order 1, so the end of the call is the last thing the voice model reads); the whole within `seed_budget` tokens (shipped 8192) by the token estimate, the oldest turns dropped first. Each `session` line is dropped once a seed carried it (a delete of each row as it was read, so a line that came in meanwhile waits for the next seed). `est_tokens` is the token estimate the budget is counted by (`estimate_tokens`, summed over the messages). The seed writes the facts it carried as `seed_facts:<live>` (`seed_fact_state`: note ids, newest inner turn, topic, the contributions to `seed_target` (`parts`), their digest), the state `./policy`'s `live_feed` compares against; `hop.live` is the ask's own handle (else `context.live`, GH #1482); without one the ask is of no running session -- one empty `seed`, `hop.refused` `no_live`, nothing read or written, the session is no fallback. Without `context.audience_set` nothing is read: `messages: []`, `hop.refused` `no_round`; a refused read `hop.degraded` `store` and the line stays -- never silence.
