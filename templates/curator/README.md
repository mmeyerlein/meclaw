# `curator@1.20.0`

The window of one model, owned in one place, with a ledger of every call. Contract tables only; the prose follows with the program it belongs to.

Since 1.1.0 ([#892](https://github.com/mmeyerlein/meclaw/issues/892), [#893](https://github.com/mmeyerlein/meclaw/issues/893), [#895](https://github.com/mmeyerlein/meclaw/issues/895), [#896](https://github.com/mmeyerlein/meclaw/issues/896)) the window follows a role (`role`: `talky`,
`consult`, `coding`, `research`), foreign words and tool results stand behind a short id
`[#<12 hex>]` the model can release or pin through the `window` section of its answer, another hive
can pin a text into the window (`in_pin`), and the hive answers the collector's menu question itself
(`./schemas`). Three cells joined it: `./history` answers the `history_*` tools out of the ledger,
`./push` builds the memory leg's question and looks a `gap` up after the answer, and `./handover`
hands a new session a note on the last one and a renewed duplex call a block of the recent
conversation. Lanes joined the boundary, so it is the second digit.

Since 1.1.1 ([#904](https://github.com/mmeyerlein/meclaw/issues/904)) the cache clock is one standing order under a fixed id per hive (per round since GH #943),
re-armed on every call (`add` with `rearm`), so the timer keeps one row for it instead of one
per call. No lane moved; a promise is repaired, so it is the third digit.

## Lanes (into the hive path, on `hop.route`)

| Lane | From | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| `in_curate` | the context collector | `messages[]` the whole running round (opening turn, evidence pairs, every tool iteration so far), never history; `system` the per-round slots; `tool_scope` if set; `hop.session_id`, `hop.turn_id`, `hop.iter` and whatever else the caller puts on the call (no session = `default`, no turn = a round of its own). An `assistant` text in a round without a tool call of its own (a duplex turn pair; the evidence pairs `memory_recall` and `affinity_brief` do not count) is a final answer and an episode. Answered by one `brain`. |
| `in_slots` | the context collector | `system` only (tool menu, sidecar contract). Kept as the collector's slots, sent with the next call whose system changed; no call of its own. |
| `in_llm` | the tap: every output of the model | the raw output with `hop.finish_reason`, `hop.model`, `hop.tokens_*`, `hop.cost`, `hop.cache_expires_at`, `hop.context_window`, the answering model's limits and prices `hop.input_soft`, `hop.input_hard`, `hop.cost_in`, `hop.cost_cached_in` (GH #1038, absent from an old package), and `context.curator_call`, `context.session_id`, `context.turn_id`, `context.iter`. A final answer (`stop`, `length`) enters the wall from here; the sentence beside a tool call (`tool_calls`) enters as an interim answer, not final, never an episode. Without a session it is session `default`, without a turn the round of its call (the rule of `brain`); without `context.curator_call` it is dropped and said on stderr. |
| `in_spec_llm` | the parent's quick voice, answering a spec call (#1275) | the quick voice's answer to a call that carried `hop.spec` "1" (policy `quick_speculative`), with the call's context (`curator_call`, `session_id`, `turn_id`) and its usage on the hop. Held in the ledger (`state` `spec_held:<session>#<turn>`), never on the wall; the normal curate of the turn keeps it or drops it (see *Speculation* below). |
| `in_spec_partial` | the parent's quick voice, streaming a spec call (#1411, GH #1415) | a partial of a spec call (hop `seq`, `final`, `partial_of`; one assistant message, cumulative), with the call's context (`curator_call`, `session_id`, `turn_id`). Never on the wall, never held, never buffered: `./policy` reads the turn's row and passes it on `partial` unchanged when the row is `await` for this call and the stored `needs` carries no `tool`; else it goes nowhere, said once on stderr (`held back (<state>)`). |
| `in_pack` | an identity pusher | `system.{identity,identity_short,persona,handover,instructions}` (or `slot` + `content`); the context rides on to `pack_ack` untouched. |
| `in_close` | the session keeper | `context.session_id` of the closed session; `context.audience_set` the round its generation was opened in, the only round the `write` batch is gated by. |
| `in_model` | the llm-registry | a params-only body for `./summarizer`, only when `hop.subscriber` names it (the one model door of the hive). |
| `in_section` | the parent's splitter | one section of the sidecar block of an answer of the model (`hop.section`, body `{messages: [], section, payload}`, the answer's context). `window` (`release`/`pin`: block ids) becomes `marks` rows; `gap` goes to `./push` (through `./policy`), which marks it once and looks it up after the answer; `memory` a `topic` mark (`{movement, name}`, movement start/continue/end) and, when `./intake` `pass_sections` names it, leaves again unchanged on `sidecar`. Any other section is dropped and said. A section whose hop carries `sidecar_verify` -- the parent splitter's rule for it, canonical JSON, GH #1079 -- and is none of `window`, `gap`, `things` is checked first and leaves on `sidecar` whatever its name: every item of its array (the rule's `items`, else the first list), each named field word for word against the round's `user`/`peer` turns (`turn`), those and the round's memory bundle (`turn+recall`) or the body's `verify_answer` (`answer`). Both sides casefolded, NFC, curly quotes and dashes folded, whitespace collapsed, punctuation at the edges stripped; the quote stands in one source at word boundaries, under three words only as a whole source; no fuzzy match. A `memory` section is checked only where `pass_sections` hands it on. |
| `in_answer_check` | the parent's splitter | GH #1306: a check of the sent answer (`answer_check`, body `{messages: [], section, verify_answer}`, the answer's context) stamped `hop.answer_check_sources` `turn+recall`. `./intake` reads the `user` and `peer` rows of the turn and the round's `recall` rows the window plan shows, through the audience gate, and hands the check on on `answer_check`; another value hands it on without sources, said on stderr. |
| `in_pin` | another hive | `pins[]`, each `{text, source, until?}`: `source` one path segment, never `model`; `until` RFC 3339. Optional `replace_sources[]` (at most 16, each by the `source` rule): every live pin of those sources ends first (`until` = now, no row deleted), except a hash the new set carries; `pins: []` with it empties those sources. A block of kind `pin` and a `pins` row; the window shows it under `system.pinned.<source>.<id>`. Nothing else of the hive is written through this door. A pin without `context.audience_set` is refused (`missing_audience`, stderr, nothing written). A pin may name its own `audience_set` (GH #972, see below). |
| `in_schemas` | the collector's menu question | `tools[]` the declared names. `./schemas` answers on `tool_schemas` with the curator's tools and sections (`CURATOR_OFFER`). |
| `in_history_call` | the parent's dispatcher, on `hop.tool_name` starting `history_` | one `history_search`, `history_read` or `history_outline` call (GH #893): one `tool_call` turn, the arguments as its text, `hop.tool_call_id`, `hop.tool_name`, the round's context. Answered by one `tool_result` out of this hive's ledger. |
| `in_recall_ask` | the context collector | its per-turn memory ask (route `recall`): `phase`, `turn_id`, `session_id`, `iter`, `recall_query`, `memory_tier`, `recall_window_from`, `recall_window_to`, the person's words as `messages[]`. Answered by exactly one `recall` (`./push`, through `./policy` for the knobs); never held back. |
| `in_gap_bundle` | the memory, through the parent's edge | the answer to a gap's own ask, recognised by `context.gap_ask`; never the collector's. A find becomes an addendum; an empty bundle, a reject or the empty-state sentence is nothing. |
| `in_renewed` | the member, from a duplex channel | a live session of a running call was renewed at its provider's limit (GH #896): `hop.call_id`, `hop.renewal_n`. Answered by one `sidecar` without a model. |
| `in_stats` | an observer, through the parent (GH #926) | a question, all hop: `stats_tag` (1 to 64 characters), `stats_from`/`stats_to` (RFC 3339, `from` counts, `to` does not, at most 31 days), `stats_kinds` (a JSON array as text out of `calls`, `gap`, `reread`, `ask`, `correction`, `tool_error`), `stats_samples` (0 to 50, default 0). Answered by exactly one `stats` out of this hive's ledger, read only. |
| `in_note` | another hive, through the parent (GH #1303) | `notes[]`, each `{id, source, text, scope, until?, priority?}`: `id` 1 to 64 characters, `source` by the rule of a pin's (never `model`), `scope` `turn`, `session` or `until`, `until` RFC 3339 (kept for `until` only), `priority` 0 to 9 (0 first, default 5); at most 32 per body, a repeated id counts by its last entry, the same id with text "" withdraws it, with or without a `scope`. One `notes` row per (`source`, `id`, round) in the round of `context.audience_set`; a body without one is refused (`missing_audience`, stderr, nothing written), a note out of its bounds alone. Nothing answers it; the window shows it (Notes, below). |
| `in_turns_ask` | `talky/route`, through the parent (GH #1304) | `{messages: [], turns_ask: {n, budget_tokens}}` and the turn's context (`session_id`, `turn_id`, `audience_set`). Answered by exactly one `turns` out of this hive's wall (`./history`); see "The person's last turns" below. A DELTA of a voice turn (`context.delta` "1", live-2 K3) stands under the call's id as `session_id` while the conversation stands under the generation's: it reads the round's newest rows whatever their session (turns, judge turns and topic), the gate of `history_read`, like `in_seed_ask`; without the delta nothing changed. |
| `in_screen_topics` | the presenter's `screen_topics`, through the parent (#1279) | `{topics: [{name, purpose, candidates?: [{key, describe}]}]}`: `./history` replaces the list it keeps (one `state` row, `topics: []` empties it; a topic without a name or a candidate without a key is left out and said). Every judge context carries it as `judge.screen.topics`. Nothing answers it. |
| `in_seed_ask` | the voice cell's `seed_ask`, through the parent (#1279) | `hop.call_id`, `hop.session_id` (the call), `hop.reason`, `hop.renewal_n`; the composition promotes `context.audience_set` and `context.call_id`. Answered by exactly one `seed` (`./history`); see "The judge context" below. |
| `in_seed_line` | a colony cell (soul2me `winddown`), through the parent (#1279) | `{session_id\|call_id, kind: wind_down\|hint\|identity, text}`: `./history` holds one line per call and kind -- `wind_down` and `hint` can stand at once, a new line replaces only its own kind of that call -- for the next `seed` of that call, which ends on every held line and drops them; `kind identity` (#1402, the runtime name) is the member's, not a call's: it needs no call (a sent `call_id`/`session_id` is ignored), stands in the head of every seed right after the facts line and holds for every call and renewal until a newer `identity` line replaces it. Nothing answers it. |
| `in_call_end` | the end of a voice call, through the parent (#1369; the set draws the voice cell's final `usage`) | `hop.call_id`, `hop.session_id`; the composition promotes `context.audience_set` and `context.call_id`. Answered by exactly one `call_facts` (`./history`), read only. |
| `in_delta_stamp` | the parent's `./route`, stamping a delta of a turn (#1279) | the stamp of the cumulative text so far (`context.delta` "1", `stamp.text_norm`), for `./policy` (`delta_speculation`): the turn's speculative curate becomes its normal curate under that stamp when the final text normalises the same, else #1275 fires anew. Since #1404 also the early crisis interrupt (`interrupt_on_delta`, with or without `delta_speculation`), addressed by the call the stamp names (`hop.call_id`, which `talky/route` carries from the voice partial, else `context.call_id`). Both off, it is parked. |

A gap's find enters the wall of the session's next round as a `memory_recall` pair whose result opens with `[addendum to your last answer -- looked up after it, for: <gap>]` -- `./push` hands the pair to `./intake`, the wall's one writer, on the internal lane `in_addendum`, once the ledger has answered the write of its `addendum_done`; the ask leaves at once, so the pair races the memory's answer to the round's first turn, and nothing orders the two; its marks are `gap` (the signal), `addendum` (the find, kept) and `addendum_done` (shown, or why not). A find the window already carries is not shown twice, and one for a session that has ended stays unshown. The gap is asked whole -- the memory hive's own query guard cuts a question (GH #1225). Whether a gap is looked up and a find kept is the ledger's word alone: the `gap` and the `addendum` mark are written only if absent, and only the message that wrote one goes on, so a twin -- after a restart of the cell too -- is looked up once and a duplex find is said once (`fact` leaves after its mark); likewise a find is shown only by the ask whose `addendum_done` landed, and a `once` candidate only by the ask whose update found it unused, so two asks in flight show each once. No timer.

**Push candidates (`in_candidate`, GH #949).** Another hive -- an installed app, a store of the person's things -- hands the curator texts it wants in the model's window of a later turn: `{source, candidates: [{id, text, audience_set?, triggers?, until?, once?, priority?}], withdraw?: [id]}`. The round is `context.audience_set` of the push and is required (`missing_audience`, nothing written); a candidate without its own `audience_set` takes the round, one with it must stay within the round (every member in the round, `*` only when the round names `*`), else that candidate alone is refused `audience_widened` (a candidate naming `[]` widens every round but `[]` itself); one out of its bounds (at most 32 per body, `id` up to 64 characters, `text` at least 1 character -- no upper bound of its own (R-IG-1), up to 16 `triggers` of up to 64 characters and none of punctuation alone, `priority` 0 to 9, default 5) is refused `invalid_input`, and a repeated `id` counts by its last entry. A kept candidate is a block of kind `candidate` and one `candidates` row per `(source, id)`: a new text, `once` or trigger list starts it afresh, `withdraw` ends it (`until` = now), and one whose `until` has passed is kept and never shown. In its `ask` phase `./push` takes the candidates the round of the ask may see (`covers`, never wider; a round-less ask only `[]` and `*`) that are live, not a used `once`, and either carry no trigger or one the person's words of this turn name as whole consecutive words (NFC, case-folded, a possessive `'s` dropped) -- and not again within `candidate_cooldown` turns (default 5). Best first by `priority`, then the youngest, they reach the model as ONE `memory_recall` pair that `./push` hands `./intake` (`in_addendum`, the road of a gap's find) for the round of the ask -- one with a `once` candidate only after the ledger answered its update, like a find: the call (id `call_candidates_<16 hex>`) asks for the triggers the person's words hit, joined with ", " (empty when none carried one), and the result is the line `[addendum -- notes handed to this conversation for this round; not said by the person]` with the part `[<text>; <text>]` below it, whole candidates within a twentieth of the window this hive serves (every picked one without a known window), the ones left out said behind the part as `...[dropped: <n> candidates (<m> chars) over budget]` and kept for a later turn (`candidate_push` "0" = off); the memory question (`recall_query`) never carries them, so every turn asks exactly as before. One ask reads at most `candidate_rows` candidate rows, best first, and a full page is said on stderr. A colony without the `candidates` table has none: the store's `unknown_table` for that read is an empty one; any other failed read of them names `a-cand` in `recall_degraded`, and the turn goes without candidates (GH #1225). `once` is true by default without triggers and false with them, and a shown `once` candidate is used. A receipt `candidate_ack` `{source, stored, refused: [{id, error_code}]}` leaves only when the push carries `hop.ack` "1": a generation fans one push out to the curator of each of its brains, and receipts nobody asked for would be dead letters. The way back out is the sidecar section `things`, offered only when `things_section` is "1" at `./schemas` and at `./intake` (the instruction is `things_instruction`): `{name, kind?, note?}` entries under `items`, as `./schemas` defines them (no count of its own since GH #1085), checked by `./intake` and handed on as ONE `thing_seen` `{items, turn_id, episode_turn_id?, session_id}` with the round in `context.audience_set` -- never without a round, and never as `sidecar` as well, because the parent's splitter hands `things` to the curator like `window`, `gap` and `memory`.

The model behind the hive orders its system part with `history` after `identity`, `persona` and `instructions` (`system_order`): the summary changes only on a rebuild and stands last. A brain `llm` with `system_writable` behind a curator must allow `history`, `pinned`, `roster`, `consult` and `instructions` -- every call carries them as `$replace` roots.

**The system part of a turn stands (GH #1298).** A brain answers one turn in several calls, one per tool round, and a provider that signs its thinking binds every block to the prefix it was made under (`system`, `tools`, the earlier messages): a replay under another prefix is refused (the native Messages wire answers 400). Two rules keep it. First, the window a call is fitted to is its OWN way's: the tap keeps `input_soft`/`input_hard` of a brain answer under the bare keys and those of any other way under `input_soft:<way>`/`input_hard:<way>` (the way rides with the call's projection `window_est:<call>`; a bridge is quick), so a quick answer no longer cuts the brain's contract, renders its earlier answers or guards its hard line. A parent with stages (`context.sa_stage`, simple-agent 2: `fast` and `strong`, two models on one window) keeps each stage's values under `<key>@<stage>` and sizes a call by its own stage's, falling back to the bare key until that stage's model has answered once; the bare `input_soft` stays the last answer's for the memory ask. Second, the FIRST `brain` call of a turn keeps its leaves, the window they were fitted to and the contract cuts it said (`state` `turn_system:<session>:<turn>`), and every later `brain` call of the same turn and round sends exactly those leaves, renders the earlier answers' sidecar blocks under that window and says those cuts -- whatever the window, a slot, a pin or a consult's mark did in between; the next turn takes them as they stand then. One row per running turn: two turns of a session keep their own, the brain's answer that ends the turn takes its row down, and a row a round left (a hand-over, a lost answer) goes with the first brain call of a later turn once it is older than `pending_sweep_s` (every row is entered in the session's list `turn_system_last:<session>` when it is written, an escalated turn's too). `tools` is not frozen: a consult the brain starts in a round denies `consult_cogny` from the next call on, the `llm` cell then asks the provider to drop the replayed thinking instead of refusing, and the `consult.pending` note joins the system with the next turn (within the turn the brain has its own call and result). Measured in production: the quick voice's answers had left its small window in state, the brain's first call cut the contract to it, the brain's own answer stamped a larger one, and the call after the tool result carried the whole contract -- the turn died after its interim sentence.

**The handover (`./handover`, GH #896).** A closed session (`in_close`) leaves a note: its turns go through `./summarizer`, off the hot path, and the answer is kept as a block of kind `summary` with a `marks` row `handover` (state `prepared`); a session that left no turn leaves nothing. The first call of a session this window did not serve last goes from `./policy` through `./handover` once, without a model: a NEW session (no wall row before this round) gets the leaf `history.handover` -- the note of the session served last, when it closed, and the topic still open, each only when the new session's round passes its audience (`state` `handover_audience` keeps the leaf's audience bound to the leaf's hash, and the window shows the leaf only when that hash is the slot's and the round passes the audience) (the `name` of the newest `topic` mark that names one, unless an `end` came after it) -- and the call returns to `./policy` (`handover-done`), which reads its window as for any call: the plan's cover, the rows it holds, every pin. The leaf carries no pins, because the window shows every valid pin already. It stands for the session and falls at the first rebuild after it of the round its audience names (GH #943). A renewed live session (`in_renewed`) sees no system part of this hive, so its block carries it all: the newest `renew_rounds` rounds as text, the summary, the pins, the open commitments (pins with `source` `commitment`) and the topic, at once and without a model. Open commitments stay empty until a hive pins them through `in_pin`; no parent draws that door yet. Every block is at most its share of the window (the window rule below), cut by rank: pins and commitments, then the topic, then the turns, the summary last. The pack family `handover` is the identity pusher's and is never touched; this leaf is `history.handover`, owner `curator`. Since GH #1225 every row the handover keeps -- the parked note, the leaf's slot, `handover_audience`, `handover_for`, `renewed_last:<session>`, `summarizer_input_soft` -- is written in one step (`insert` with `replace` on its key, a block `if_absent` on its hash), so a refused write leaves the old value, never none. Its reads name what they look for, with no count: the newest summary the round may see and the wall above its cover, the newest `topic` mark that says something (however many quiet turns came since), the `handover` marks of the one session in question. A read the ledger refused is never "no rows": the step reads once more, and refused again it ends loudly -- a `store_error` mark, the code in the header -- with no note, no leaf for a returning session, and a renewal block that carries its head only. Whether a session is prepared is decided by a `prepared` mark read from the ledger, never by what the child sent.

## The window per role

`./policy` `role` picks the presets; every knob set on the instance wins. The window is the system part (slots, `pinned.*`, stubs) + the wall after the plan's cover, each block as the plan shows it + the running round. A rebuild (cold cache, a line of the model's package -- below --, or without a package `tokens_prompt` over `compress_at` of the model's own window) makes the plan: segments by `horizon`, their form by `tiers` (`raw`, `summary`, `none`), the newest `keep_recent` rounds and the newest round always raw, the model's pins raw. The running round always goes out whole -- the person's words and every call/result pair -- and a cut before it never separates a tool call from its result, in older rounds neither: the role `quick` counts its `keep_recent` 4 in elements before the running round, and the cut moves back to the call of every result it would orphan (GH #1347: counted over the whole window, a round of three calls side by side lost the question and two calls). Over `rebuild_to` of the usable window it shrinks old tool results (outside `keep_rounds`), then stubs tools unused for `stub_tools_after` rounds, then gives the summary up. Between two rebuilds only the end of the window moves.

**No window of a role's own (R-IG-1, GH #1085, since 1.11.3).** The usable window is the catalog row of the answering model: `input_soft`, which the llm cell stamps on every answer (GH #1037), cut only to the model's own reported window. Until 1.11.2 a role capped it (`quality_cap`: talky 120000, consult 80000, coding 70000, research 80000 tokens, GH #892) -- a talky under luna (`input_soft` 250000) kept 120000, handed that to every producer and, on an answer without the row's limits, rebuilt at 60000. Whether a model reads a smaller window well is a question of its catalog row, never a knob of this hive.

**Short ids.** With `short_ids` on, somebody else's words and a tool result begin with `[#<12 hex>] `, the first 12 hex digits of the wall block's hash; the model's own answers and tool calls never do. The model names ids in its `window` section: `release` shows the block as `[#<id> released — history_read("#<id>")]` from the next rebuild on, `pin` keeps it raw below the cover. Identity, persona, instructions and the round the section was said in stay; an unknown id is ignored and said. A block with `valid_until` passed at the plan's time shows as `[#<id> expired]`. A model may quote an id in another bracket, a footnote mark `[^<id>]` and its definition line among them; whatever leaves toward a reader (`./push`, `./handover`, and the parent's splitter) loses it by form, the same one expression in every copy ([#1345](https://github.com/mmeyerlein/meclaw/issues/1345)). The ledger keeps every block byte for byte; a form the window shows is a block of kind `view`.

**Window plan per round (GH #943).** `./policy` keeps one window plan per round, not one per hive: with one plan, a rebuild one round started set the cut, the shrunk results and the stubs of every other round's window. A round's key `round_key` is the first 12 hex digits of the SHA-256 of the canonical round (the JSON array sorted, without duplicates, as `audience_set` carries it); a call without a round has the key of `[]`, and `["*"]` is a round of its own. Under it `state` holds `window_plan:<rk>`, the rebuild claim `rebuild_running:<rk>` (one rebuild per round at a time), `last_call:<rk>`, `armed_call:<rk>`, `actions_pending:<rk>`, `pending:summary:<rk>` and `summary_audience:<rk>`, and `./intake` hands a call on with the plan of its own round. The summary slot is `history.summary:<rk>`, shown to its round as the leaf `history.summary` and to no other round. The cache clock holds one order per round (`schedule_id` = uuid5 of the policy's namespace and `cache:<rk>`), so a call of one round never re-arms another round's order. `state` `window_plans` maps each round key to the time of its last rebuild; every rebuild stamps its own round and deletes the state of the rounds idle for longer than `round_plan_idle_s` (default 2592000 s, 30 days), then of the oldest beyond `max_round_plans` (default 64) -- never its own; what a dropped round owns falls with its plan (GH #949): its state rows including `summary_audience:<rk>` and its summary slot, deleted only after the registry write that drops it was won, row by row against the value read. The first call of a returning round whose newest visible summary is its own lays its leaf, slot and binding afresh from that `summaries` row; a cache order of the round that is still armed falls too -- the timer is told `remove` for the round's id (it marks the row and keeps it), an order that struck already gets none, and a strike before the removal finds no `last_call:<rk>`. A handover leaf bound to no audience reaches no round and falls at the next rebuild of any round. The ledger declares `state.key` unique (`state_key`), so the first write of a round's key is an insert only one writer wins, and a lost registry write is read and put again (at most three times). The bare keys of the one plan per hive (`window_plan`, `rebuild_running`, `last_call`, `armed_call`, `actions_pending`, `pending:summary`, `summary_audience`, a bare `history.summary` slot) are neither read nor moved: every round starts its plan afresh. `round_key`, `round_canon`, `own_slots` and `prune_plans` are pure functions like those below.

**Reading the round (`in_read`, GH #949).** An installed app reads the ledger of its round on `in_read`, answered on `read` with `hop.read_tag` echoed: `what` `turns` pages the wall rows its round may see (oldest first from the cursor `since`, `limit` 20 by default and 100 at most, `next` empty at the end, within a tenth of the reader's window, `hop.input_soft` of the read -- never the window this hive serves, which is another model's -- every row whole when the read names none; a first row larger than that is cut with the mark of the window rule below and flagged `cut`), `sessions` lists the sessions with their last time. The round is `context.audience_set` and nothing else, matched through `covers` like every reader of the ledger: a read without a round, or with `[]`, is answered `missing_audience` and reads nothing, a row reaches the read only when its audience holds the round, and a failed store read is `store_error`, never an empty page. The way in is `install_app` `reads`, which names the app's cell and the brain by `hop.organ`; a run the timer wakes carries no round and is refused.

**The price of the window (GH #1038).** A model's limits differ per model, and a cached window is not free: at a cached price of 10 % of the input price, a 500k window costs on every turn what 50k fresh tokens cost. The llm cell stamps the answering model's catalogue values on every answer (GH #1037): `input_soft` and `input_hard` in tokens, `cost_in` and `cost_cached_in` in cents per million tokens. With `input_soft` on the tap they replace the role's own numbers -- `compress_at` of the model's window orders nothing: a rebuild is due at `input_soft` (`curate_mark` `soft`), over `input_hard` (`hard`; the llm cell refuses such a call itself), and under soft when keeping the cached rest costs more over `amortise_turns` turns than one rebuild, `(window - aim) * cost_cached_in * N > aim * cost_in` (`cost`). The aim of a `soft` or `hard` rebuild is `input_soft` times `rebuild_to` / `compress_at` (the share of the window W11 made soft keeps its proportion), `input_soft` itself for a `hard` rebuild of a role without one; a `cost` rebuild aims lower, at `input_soft` times `rebuild_to` -- with the soft share as its aim the rule would need a cached price above 23 % (talky) of the input price and never fire, with this one it is due under soft from 0.7 (talky, consult) or 0.5 (coding, research) of `input_soft` at a cached price of 10 % and ten turns. The rule is conservative: it counts the rebuild at `cost_in` in full, neither the cached part the rebuilt call itself still reads nor a provider's cache-write price (`cost_cache_write`). Worked example: `cost_in` 10 cents per million, cached 10 % of it, a 500k window rebuilt to 100k -- keeping it costs 0.4 cents a turn more, the rebuild 1 cent once, so it pays back on the third turn. Every turn keeps `{call, curate_mark, window_tokens, cached_tokens, cost_cents, input_soft, input_hard}` under `state` `curate:<rk>`, and a turn with the package's values writes the same as a `marks` row of kind `curate`; the clock order of such a rebuild carries the mark as its `reason` and as `curate_mark`. The window is measured, never summed: after a `length` continuation the llm cell reports `tokens_prompt` as the sum of every call of the answer, so the package's rule takes the smaller of that and the policy's own projection of the call (every system leaf and every message, kept as `state` `window_est:<call>` until the tap -- a call no tap answers loses it to the next call of its round, and the round's pointer `est_call:<rk>` falls with the round --, estimated like the llm cell estimates a request: UTF-8 bytes of every string and key / 3, an inline image 1024 tokens). And because the cell refuses over `input_hard` on that estimate, `hard` is also due when the estimate of the next window -- this call plus its answer -- reaches 0.9 of `input_hard`; the tenth is headroom for what the projection does not see (the provider request's framing, the next turn, a tool result). Without `input_soft` (an old package) the rule of before holds and the mark is `none`.

**The role's own lines (GH #1272).** A conversation's window reached the model's soft line practically never (luna 250000), so a role's `tiers`, `keep_rounds` and day horizon never applied and whole days lay raw in the window. Two more marks order a rebuild under soft, package or not: `target` when the window reaches the role's `window_target` (tokens; talky 40000, quick 12000, 0 = off, every instance may set its own), and `day` for a role with `horizon` `day` when the turn is on another UTC day than the newest round of the round's last rebuild (`state` `last_rebuild_day:<rk>`, written by every rebuild of such a role; a round that never rebuilt starts at the day of its first answer). The turn's day is its own time where `./intake` stamped one (a replay channel's `happened_at`, the clock the rows age by), the tap's clock otherwise. Both aim at `window_target` times `rebuild_to` / `compress_at`, never above a soft rebuild's aim (`day` of a role without a target at the soft aim). A `target` rebuild that leaves the window over the target (the rounds kept raw alone are that large) is not repeated on every turn: the round keeps the window of its last `target` order (`state` `target_at:<rk>`), lowered on every later turn to that turn's window when it is smaller, so the row holds the window the rebuild left; the next `target` waits until the window has grown by `window_target` minus the aim from there, or has fallen under the target; until then the mark is `none` and the turn's `curate` stamp says `target_hold`. The order is `hard`, `soft`, `day`, `target`, `cost`. A `day` rebuild keeps raw only the `keep_recent` rounds of the running day, so the day before takes its tier form (talky: the summary) -- its tool results included -- and today stays raw.

**A refused read (GH #1225).** A ledger leg the store refused is never read as an empty answer. A call's window reads once more (the reason carries `reread:`; the normal path takes no extra hop); a second refusal sends the call with its system part and its round, without what is missing, and says so: one `cuts` entry per missing part (`what` `wall`, `slots` or `pins`, `shown` 0, `error_code`, `legs`) and one `store_error` mark. A hash read refused twice (`b-wallh`, `b-keeph`, `b-slotsh`, `b-pins`, the parked turn `b-slimq`, or the cover `b-cover` of a call whose plan holds none) is the same cut of the part it was read for, never a `missing_block` for a block that stands. When `./intake` could not read a turn's plan or cover, the parked turn names those reads (`wall_unread`, `a-plan`/`a-cover`): the call shows its own turn only -- no older row, no `missing_block` -- with one `cuts` entry `{what: wall, error_code: store_error, legs: <the reads>}`; the `store_error` mark is `./intake`'s. With its slots unknown a call sends no system diff and stamps no `system_hash_sent`; a parked turn out of reach twice leaves no call, only the mark and a line on stderr. A rebuild step stops with the mark: a refused probe starts no rebuild, a refused claim is no lost race, a refused question or answer read frees the claim by its token. A refused tap read writes nothing it would derive from the answer's usage, only the round's last call and the mark. Every state row, block and model pin is written in one step (`insert` with `replace` on the key, `if_absent` on the hash, or on hash, source and audience for a pin), so a refused write never loses the row it replaces; a refused window-form write empties the child's form cache, and the next call puts every form again.

## The ways of a call (`path_routes`, GH #1247)

The route cell of the composite stamps every turn (`talk`, `simple`, `complex`, `research`); the collector carries the path on the seam as `hop.path` and the parent's edge lifts it into `context.path`. The stamp recommends, `./policy` decides (R-WP-1): `path_routes` names the way the call leaves on. `quick` is the short window of the role `quick` with the class's tools (`quick_tools`) callable and the stamp's other families as hints (R-WM-1: the curator owns what the model sees); `consult` and `research` are one mechanism (R-WP-9): the whole errand leaves for the backend under its own `consult_id`, without a call of the voice model (R-WP-5), and the session's speaking calls carry `system.consult.pending` until the round that answers it -- recognised by `consult_id` on its context -- leaves on `brain` and takes the leaf down. The state row `consult_pending:<session>` holds the open consult; its `until` is the errand's stamp plus `consult_pending_ms`, for `research` plus `research_wait_ms` as well (GH #1260: the result comes in a later turn, after the wait the bridge names). **While it stands, no speaking call can start a second consult (GH #1280).** Every `brain` and `quick` call of the session denies the consult tool `consult_cogny` in its `tool_scope` (added to the channel's own `deny`; a scope whose `allow` does not hold the tool stays as it is), and the pending note stays beside it as information: told only in the note not to consult again, the brain of a `talk` turn consulted again anyway, as a tool call without a word beside it (production log of 2026-10-20: a second consult 9 s after the first, about 0.09 $). A consult the brain starts itself sets the same mark: `./intake` takes the ids of the `consult_cogny` calls in the model's output into the tap (`consults`, the folded form `consult{op: "cogny"}` too), and `./policy` writes the mark in its phase `tap` under the consult id the dispatcher stamps -- a follow-up's `consult_id` argument, else the call's id; cogny's advice comes back under it -- only while none stands, so a consult the curator sent keeps its own. With the `consult` family folded (`context.tools_folded`) the menu entry is `consult`, and the deny names it as well. The advice ends the mark and gives the tool back; an ask back of the backend (`consult_class` `ask`) does not, the consult still runs. `consult_pending_ms` is only the backstop for an advice that never comes.

**The speaker after a tool result (T4-10, GH #1248).** A quick round that called a tool comes back on the seam with the result (`iter` >= 1), and `./policy` names the speaker again: the quick voice keeps it only while the result is speakable as it is -- every tool call of the turn has its result (no open gap), the newest result is neither empty nor an error object (`error`/`error_code`), and the round is at most `quick_max_iter` iterations past its first call. Anything else leaves on `brain` with ONE `escalate` mark (`{call, path, from: quick, to: brain, reason, iter}`, reason `iterations`, `gap` or `unspeakable`), and the state row `escalated:<session>` keeps the turn with the brain for the rest of its round. The brain's call is the whole window: the quick way cleared `system_hash_sent`, so its tree goes whole.

**The bridge (T4-12, GH #1248).** A `consult` or `research` errand never leaves the person in silence (R-WP-4, R-TR-27): beside it `./policy` sends the quick voice ONE call with the leaf `instructions.bridge` -- an instruction, never a fixed sentence; the model says it in its own words (R-WP-4 a). On both ways the instruction tells the voice that it is handling the request for the person right now, in its own voice, and that it never refuses the request or says it cannot do something (GH #1258): told only that a backend was working, it once denied in its own name the very request the backend was answering. For `complex` (way `consult`) it says one short bridge sentence and the answer follows in the same turn within about `bridge_wait_ms` (R-WP-9); for `research` it names the waiting time (`research_wait_ms`) and keeps the conversation going, and the result comes in a later turn as a round with `consult_id` that leaves on `brain`. The bridge calls no tool and carries no consult id; the errand carries its mark as `hop.consult_wait_ms` -- read by no cell, a mark for whoever reads the message log -- and the state row `consult_pending:<session>` keeps it as `wait_ms`. The bridge is a model call of its own with its own `calls` row (`trigger` `bridge`, GH #1260), so its tap books its usage. **The tool round has a bridge too (GH #1260, OR-WG-6).** A `simple` turn whose stamp needs a `tool` sends its quick call with `tool_scope.require` -- in the wave's lab the fast model called its tool in 0 of 5 turns and 7 of 10 probe calls, 10 of 10 only when the call was required, and then said nothing beside it -- so the bridge (`hop.bridge` `tool`) says the sentence the person hears while the lookup runs, told not to answer yet. It carries `hop.interim` `1`, which talky lifts into the context of the quick call and the dispatcher puts on its sentence: an interim answer leaves the round open for the tool result, a final one would end it at the fan-in. The bridge leaves BEFORE the required call, on the same edge: both go to the one `./quick`, a stateful `llm` cell that takes its mailbox one call at a time (`max_concurrency` is a knob of stateless cells), so the two cannot run side by side, and in this order the person hears the bridge after one model call, not two. **A tool round without its call goes to the brain (GH #1260, OR-WG-9).** The required call carries `hop.interim` `tool`. When its answer comes back without a tool call -- prose, an error such as the HTTP 404 of a host that refuses a forced call, or a cut -- the dispatcher does not say it, `./intake` keeps its prose out of the wall, and `./policy` (`tap`) takes the round on as the call `<call>-esc` on `brain`, from the copy of the parked turn `win` kept (`pending:<call>-esc`, dropped when the call came), with ONE `escalate` mark, reason `no_tool_call`, which names how the call ended (`finish`) and its `error` code; `escalated:<session>` keeps the turn with the brain. talky's `./quick -> ./errors` edge does not take the error of such a call (`context.interim` `tool`): the person hears the brain's answer, not an error report beside it. talky's `./curator -> ./brain` edge sets `context.interim` empty, so the brain's answer is the turn's final one. **A quick call without an answer goes to the brain too (GH #1352).** Every quick call the curator sends keeps the same copy of its parked turn; when its answer is an error (a `tool_call_limit` cut, a timeout, a provider error) or a content filter, `tap` takes the round on as `<call>-esc` on `brain` with ONE `escalate` mark, reason `no_answer`, naming `finish` and the `error` code, and every other end drops the copy. The call is known as a quick call by that copy too, not only by its `window_est:<call>` projection (#1382): a call whose projection the next call of the round or of the session's next turn took down still goes to the brain on its error -- with speculation on or off -- until the pending sweep (`pending_sweep_s`, 600 s by default) takes the copy; a call that hangs longer than that, up to its own `external_timeout_ms`, ends in silence as before. In a lab run the quick voice streamed a 17th call over the dispatcher's budget, the cell stopped with `provider_error`, and the person got no answer at all. A bridge keeps no copy: its errand answers the turn. Every bridge states no content: its instruction forbids facts, numbers, results and guesses (a lab bridge stated a rain forecast before any lookup); the tool round's says only that it is looking it up, the consult's that the answer is still coming, and research names its wait. The tool round's bridge also names the lookup that runs beside it: the tools its `tool_scope` allows at the ops stage (not the hint stage), a family once, in words: where each looks ("on the web and at the weather service"; a family without words for its place is never named by its tool name -- beside named ones it adds "in other places", alone the sentence names no place), and it is told to write no tool call, JSON, code or command -- told the `code` name of its tool, the fast model wrote the call out as text and made weather up beside it in a probe. And every bridge is told never to speak of missing access, missing abilities or limits and to ask nothing about the request itself (GH #1260, OR-WG-13): a lab bridge, not knowing a tool worked for it, said it had no access to weather data and asked which day, while the `weather` call ran. The tool round's one sentence asks the person nothing at all, the consult's asks nothing about the request itself, and research keeps the conversation going while it waits (R-TR-27) -- on another subject, never on the request: told only that anything else was fine, it asked in a probe what mattered to the person in the request. Its window carries no `tool_call` or `tool_result` of earlier turns; on a consult and on a tool round it is the person's turn alone -- every text of the person since the last message that is not theirs, so a request sent in two messages stays whole, and never a pin standing as a message (`broadcast_mode` tail), which the window can put behind the turn (research keeps the quick window): an earlier answer in the window is a pattern the fast model follows -- in a probe it answered a consult in the bridge itself and said tonight's degrees on a tool round, and with the person's turn alone it did neither. The curator does not see the menu of the quick model: when the channel's scope names tools that menu does not carry, the `llm` cell drops the requirement (its `WARN` line), and the answer of that round comes without its sidecar block, after the bridge's interim sentence.

**Notes (`in_note`, GH #1303).** A pin stands in the system part, changes the cached head and lives until its `until`; a candidate is shown once, on a trigger, as a recall pair. A hint that has to stay near the newest turn for a whole conversation -- drift protection -- is a note. The window of a speaking way (`brain`, `quick`; a consult's errand and a bridge carry none) shows the live notes its round passes, and the turn's own `context.turn_notes` (`{id, source, text, priority?}`, scope `turn`, never a `notes` row -- the way a cell earlier in the same turn hands one on), as ONE block that opens the newest message of the person, in that element: `[notes for this turn -- from <sources>; guidance, not something the person said]`, then one line `- <text>` per note, a blank line, the person's words; what moves into the message later -- a folded recall (GH #1333), the session boundary -- follows the block. Never an element of its own and never the system part: the provider's cache mark stands on the block right before the youngest user text, so an element of its own would carry the mark and move the cached prefix every turn; inside the person's element every byte before it is the window without a note. Order: `priority`, then `turn` before `session` before `until`. Over `notes_share` of the usable window the last of that order goes first (a window not known yet shows every note; a share of 0 none). A `turn` note belongs to the turn whose window first saw it (`used_turn`, shown or left out), a `session` note ends with the close of its session (`in_close`, the session it came in, or any session of its round when it named none), an `until` note at its time or its withdrawal. Every call of one brain turn shows the block of the turn's first call (GH #1298: the person's turn belongs to the prefix a signed thinking is bound to; the block rides in `turn_system:<session>:<turn>`), the next turn takes the notes as they stand. Every window with notes leaves one `marks` row `notes` (`{call, notes_in, notes_dropped}`, `{id, source, scope}` each, never a text) and, when it left one out, a `cuts` entry `{what: notes, shown, total, unit: notes}`. Notes are no wall rows: no summary, no day tier, no `write` batch, no history tool and no memory sees them. The person's element that opens with the block is kept with its call in the ledger like every element sent (`blocks`, kind `view`), so the call stays rebuildable; the wall never holds it. A ledger without the `notes` table holds none.

**The session boundary (GH #1335).** The window reads the wall over every session (OR-KX-K4), so once another session stands in it, every user text of a session whose newest row lies before the start of the running one opens with `[earlier session, since <start> -- from before this session, not said now]`, every user text of a session that overlaps it (another channel's open session) with `[other session, since <start>]`, and the first user text of the running session with `[this session, since <start>]`; `<start>` is the earliest row the wall still holds of that session (after a rebuild covered its opening, the first row after the cover; the running session's start moves the same way, so an overlapping session can then read as earlier). Text only, from session ids and row times, never from the words: the order, the pairs and the person's turn as the last message stay.

**The person's frame (GH #1337).** Every block the window puts in front of the person's words in their element -- the notes block, a recall or brief folded into the turn that opens the window -- carries a frame, so the words get one too: whenever anything but their own session boundary stands before them, the person's words open with `[the person, now -- their own words to you]` on a line of its own, between their short id (with short ids on) and the words; in an element of an earlier round -- a turn of the past that opened the window with a folded recall -- the frame is `[the person -- their own words]`, never "now". A bare session boundary, or the words alone, leave the element as it was. A text the rebuild turned into its one-line pointer (released, expired) is no words and gets no frame. Text only, from what stands before the words in the element, never from the words; it runs last, after the fold, the notes block and the session boundary. The newest element stands behind the provider's cache mark, and an element of an earlier round carries its past frame from the next turn on, byte for byte the same.

**Speculation (`quick_speculative`, #1275).** The route stamp is a model call of its own, and the quick voice used to wait for it. With the knob on, the collector sends the same curate of a turn early -- `hop.spec` "1", no path -- and `./policy` asks the quick voice at once: way `quick`, `tool_scope` = `spec_tools` only, no tool round, no `route` mark, no `-esc` copy. The answer comes back on `in_spec_llm` and is held. Each turn has one state row `spec:<session>#<turn>`; the first of the two curates claims it (insert-if-absent), every later move is a compare-and-set, so a turn never gets two answers or none, also across a restart. The normal curate of the turn decides:

| The normal curate finds | Way of the turn | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| no row (it came first) | any | its own call, as without speculation; a later spec curate makes no call |
| answer held | not `quick` (`talk`, `complex`, `research`, or no stamp) | spec dropped, its own call goes |
| answer held | `quick`, the answer called a tool (every stamped family, when the stamp names them), or the turn needs none | kept: `spec_release` + tap, no call of its own |
| answer held | `quick`, `tool` in `needs`, the answer called none | dropped, its own call goes (the tool round with `require`) |
| answer held | `quick`, `tool` in `needs`, a stamped family outside `spec_tools` or not called by the answer (#1366) | dropped (reason `family:<name>`), its own call goes with the full quick scope |
| answer not in yet | not `quick` | spec dropped, its own call goes; the late answer is discarded |
| answer not in yet | `quick`, `tool` in `needs`, a stamped family outside `spec_tools` (#1398) | dropped at once (reason `family:<name>`), its own call and its bridge go now; the late answer is discarded once (`late_spec_discarded`) |
| answer not in yet | `quick`, otherwise | its own call is built and stored (`spec_fb:<session>#<turn>`), nothing sent; on arrival keep -> release, drop -> the stored call goes |

A failed answer (`error`, `content_filter`) is no answer: dropped, or the stored call goes; its `quick_spec` mark carries `spec_error` `{finish, error_code}` (#1382). When the stored call fails as well (error or timeout), the turn goes to the brain once (`escalate`, reason `no_answer`, GH #1352) -- also when it comes back after the session's next call took its projection (`window_est:<call>`): its `-esc` copy still names it a quick call (#1382). Nothing of a dropped answer ever reaches the splitter, the wall, the episodes or the history, and a tool it called never runs. One mark `quick_spec` per decided turn: `{call, fired_ms, done_ms, verdict: kept|dropped, reason, cost}` -- `cost` the spec call's own (null when the answer was not in at the decision). **Every spec answer is booked (#1279).** Its usage (`model`, `tokens_*`, `cost`, `cache_expires_at`) goes into the spec call's `calls` row on arrival, in the same bundle that holds it -- kept or dropped, the money is spent and the curator knows it (R-HK-14); the tap of a kept answer writes the same values again. **The rows go at their end (#1279), by event, never a timer.** The row `spec:<session>#<turn>` is deleted where its turn is decided -- kept (`f-srel`), dropped by the normal curate (`f-sdrop`), settled after `await` (by key and the value read), or dropped by its own failed answer -- and the bare `normal` claim goes in the bundle of the normal call it guarded (`f-snorm`): from there the turn's `calls` row is the guard, a spec curate reads it beside its claim (`w-spec-c`) and, finding the turn called, parks and takes its own claim back. Not when its own row is `await` (#1371): that `calls` row is the stored fallback waiting for this spec answer -- the normal curate came while the spec curate was in `./handover` -- so the spec call goes and its answer settles the turn. When a kept answer settles an `await`, the stored call never went, so its `calls` and `call_blocks` rows, its `pending:<call>-esc` copy and its `window_est:<call>` go with it (the `route` mark stays: the turn's path and way were what it says). A spec answer arriving after its row went is discarded as before and leaves one mark `late_spec_discarded` `{call, finish, cost}` (#1398): nothing is sent on it. **A kept speculation streams (#1411, GH #1415).** A partial of the spec call comes in on `in_spec_partial`; `./policy` reads the turn's row and its stored fallback once and passes the partial on `partial` unchanged only when the row is `await` for this call and `needs` carries no `tool` -- then the verdict keeps every answer, so no sentence speaks that another answer would follow. `fired` or `held` (no stamp yet), `dropped`, `released`, another call's row or none: it goes nowhere (one stderr line). Partials are cumulative, so a held-back sentence comes with the next one or with the answer; nothing is buffered, written or compared-and-set. **Its own cell (#1398).** The spec call leaves as way `quick` with `hop.spec` "1" and talky routes it to `./quick_spec`, a cell with no road to the splitter; with the knob on, a bridge carries `hop.spec` "bridge" and goes to `./quick_bridge`, so neither queues behind the other or behind the tool round on `./quick`. Off, no call carries either value.

**Clarify (#1279 Change 2/3).** A set whose route asks the class (route `clarify` "1") may stamp a turn `clarify`: its words can be meant in more than one way even with the context. Such a turn leaves on `brain` whatever `path_routes` says -- no quick voice, a spec answer dropped (reason `path:clarify`), `tool_scope` `{allow: []}` with the channel's deny kept (a question calls nothing and opens nothing on a screen), and no `turn_system:` row (a turn without tools has no tool rounds to freeze its system part over). Its window carries ONE turn note `clarify_note` (source `curator`, priority 0, under `notes_share` like every note): one short question how the person means it, nothing guessed. The turn's first curate keeps the row `clarify:<session>` (`{turn, call, at}`). The first curate of the person's next turn in that session (iteration 0, no spec, no escalation) that carries their words takes the row by its key and the value read, in a bundle of its own (phase `clarify-pair`), and only when that take affected the row does ONE `propose` leave: the question is the assistant's final answer to the clarify turn on the wall (its sidecar block left out), the answer the person's words of this turn, the class the path this turn's stamp resolved to (empty without one) -- exactly once per clarify turn, also when two chains of the turn run. A turn without the person's words (an inner turn) leaves the pair waiting; a clarify turn whose answer is not on the wall, or a round without exactly one entry that is no `agent:` (the person, `entity_ref`), takes the row without a proposal (said on stderr). When the next turn is itself `clarify`, its row is put in the same bundle after the take. Without a `clarify` stamp nothing is written; each first curate reads the one key `clarify:<session>`.

**The quick voice's own prompt and its moment (#1279).** The brain reads the whole `instructions` family, so a slot `instructions.quick` would be its prompt too: the brain never gets it (its frozen turn system included), whatever the switch. With `quick_instructions_slot` "1" the slot is the quick voice's instructions -- on the quick way, a spec call and a bridge it takes the place of the other `instructions` leaves (the brain's prompt) and of the fixed leaf of GH #1248; the sidecar contract and the session's own slots stay, and the tool round keeps it (it is the voice's prompt, not the sentence beside the call). `0` leaves the quick voice's leaves as before. With `place` or `timezone` set, every call of `./quick` carries the slot `frame`: `[now and place -- facts of this moment, not something the person said]`, `now: <YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM>, <weekday> (<zone>)` from the call's own stamp in `timezone` (UTC when empty or unknown), `place: <place>`. Facts only, never an instruction; both empty: no slot.

**The spec curate as the turn's call, from a delta (#1279, `delta_speculation`).** A voice turn is stamped while the person still speaks: `talky/route` decides each partial (the cumulative text) and its stamp comes in on `in_delta_stamp` with `text_norm`; `./policy` keeps the newest per turn in the state row `delta:<turn>`. When the turn's spec curate comes (#1275), the person's words of the turn on the wall are normalised the same way (collapsed whitespace, lower case, trailing `.!?…,;:` and space cut). The same text, the spec curate's own claim (`fired`) and no call of the turn yet: the curate IS the turn's normal curate -- no `spec`, the delta's `path`, `needs`, `families` and turn notes on its seam -- and is built like one (way, tool round, notes, `route` mark). Its call is stored in `delta_fb:<session>#<turn>` and the row `spec:<session>#<turn>` moves `fired` -> `delta` by CAS in the same bundle; phase `delta-send` sends it only when the move took (mark `delta` `{turn_id, verdict: reused, call}`). The turn's normal curate then finds the row `delta` and parks -- no second call -- and takes the row and the delta row with it. If the normal curate came in between and stored its call as the fallback of an awaited spec answer (`await`), the move fails: the delta call never goes (its records go), the stored call does, mark `delta` `refired` with `reason` `lost`; a normal curate whose `await` lost to a `delta` row parks the same way. Another text: #1275 as before -- the spec call fires, mark `delta` `refired`. No delta: #1275 unchanged. A delta row goes with the spec curate that read it, or at the turn's tap. Off (the default), a delta stamp is parked (stderr) and nothing is written.

**What the voice hears after a turn (#1279, `live_feed`, through `renew`).** A duplex call runs on a provider session that keeps its own context. `./policy` notes the turn of a duplex call where it builds the turn's call (the seam carries `engine` "duplex" and `call_id`; state row `live_turn:<session>#<turn>`). When the turn closes -- a final answer (no `tool_calls`, no error, no tool round, no bridge) on `brain` or `quick` -- the tap reads that row (phase `live`), then the facts a seed of that call carries, read the way `./history` reads them for the seed, and the facts of the call's last seed or feed, `seed_facts:<call>` (phase `live-facts`): the session and until notes the round sees, the newest inner turn (`advice`, `delegation`, `event`), the newest NAMED topic (a nameless `continue` mark is no topic, an `end` mark ends it -- `topic_now`, the seed's rule), the screen topics. When their digest (`seed_fact_state`, one text in `./policy` and `./history`) moved, ONE `live_feed` leaves -- `hop.section` `live_feed_section` (`renew`, with `hop.at_turn_end` `"1"`: the voice cell renews at the next turn boundary and then asks for the seed), `hop.call_id` -- saying what moved: `notes` (`{source, id, text}`), `inner_turns` (`{turn_id, kind}`), `topic`, `screen_topics` (names). Facts, never an instruction; the facts themselves reach the voice through the next seed, which carries every one of them (the notes as a `Notes:` line, an inner turn as a `(<kind>) <text>` line) and writes `seed_facts:<call>` itself. Nothing moved, nothing is sent; a text turn has no row and sends nothing; a call without a seed or feed compares against nothing.

**When the answer comes (#1400, `horizon_advise`, section `horizon`).** On the phone the live model keeps the talk going while a round runs; told how long it will take, it fills the wait in its own words. A duplex call's turn that gets a tool round or a delegation -- the turns with a bridge: the tool round of a `simple` turn, way `consult` or `research`; never a turn answered from the seed, never a text turn, never the spec curate, only iteration 0 -- sends ONE `live_feed` with `hop.section` `horizon`, no `hop.at_turn_end` (the voice says it at once on a quiet line; otherwise it holds it to the first quiet moment -- no spoken section open, the model's own audio `spoken_quiet_ms` past, the audio queue empty -- at the latest `spoken_cap_ms` after it came, never into running speech; a newer horizon replaces a held one; a horizon still held when the answer goes out, as a new spoken section or as an append that answers a delegation, lapses: said after it, it would announce what the caller has already heard), `hop.call_id`, `hop.session_id`, `hop.turn_id`, and a body of numbers: `seconds` (rounded up), `way`, `family`. Never words: the sentence is the voice's `duplex.gpt_live.horizon_text`. The seconds are the `horizon_quantile` (nearest rank, default 75, never the median) of the rounds of the turn's family booked so far, else `horizon_defaults_ms[family]`, else nothing is sent; several families take the longest, a turn without a family counts under its way. The horizon rides with the round's own sends: stored with them for an awaited spec answer or a delta, sent with them, gone when the round never goes -- its `horizon_sent` mark and its turn row are written only when the sends go. The turn row `pending:horizon:<session>#<turn>` (`family`, `way`, `at_ms`, `seconds`, `parked_at`) times the round; a turn that never closes leaves it to the A13 sweep of parked rows; when the turn closes (the live feed's rule: a final answer on `brain` or `quick`), phase `hz-close` books its duration under its family in ONE state row `horizon_rounds` (family -> the newest 20 durations in ms) and takes the turn row. Marks `horizon_sent {seconds, family}` and, at the answer, `horizon_hit {planned, actual}` (seconds). Off (the default), nothing of this is read, written or sent.

**A crisis heard on a partial is asked at once (#1404, `interrupt_on_delta`, section `interrupt`).** The guard asks its crisis question at the turn's end; on the phone that came 1.0 to 1.8 s after the sentence, while `talky/route` had already judged the partials. With `interrupt_on_delta` on, the first delta stamp (`in_delta_stamp`) of a voice turn whose `choices.risk` value has a question in `interrupt_questions` sends ONE `live_feed` with `hop.section` `interrupt` at once -- no `hop.at_turn_end`, `hop.call_id`, `hop.session_id`, `hop.turn_id`, the question as the assistant's text (the voice: an instruction and a spoken commentary). The judgement is the route's: a value without a question, a value of source `fallback` never interrupts, nor does a fallback stamp that is no judgement of the text (`hop.fallback` `no_text`, `error`, `late`: mark `interrupt_skipped {reason: fallback:<value>}`). Since GH #1407 a stamp of `hop.fallback` `unsure` interrupts: that value marks the route's path only, its choices stay the model's (#1304). The call comes from `hop.call_id` (the route carries it from the voice partial), else `context.call_id`; a partial with neither sends nothing and books the mark `interrupt_skipped {reason: no_call_id}` -- the guard at the turn's end stays the net. At most one per turn: the claim `pending:interrupt:<turn>` (`if_absent`, `parked_at` for the A13 sweep) lets the advise leave only where it took. The turn's end (its normal curate) sends none and books `interrupt_early {delta_n, ms_before_turn_end}` (`delta_n`: the words of the partial it went on). The guard's own interrupt of the same turn is a no-op at the voice (it deduplicates by `hop.turn_id`). Off (the default), nothing of this is read, written or sent.

**The turn path waits for its delegation (#1407).** On the phone the live model delegated the person's turn, and beside the delegation round the turn's own path went on: a second tool call, then the quick voice's second round ran 20 s into its timeout. A delegation round (`./assemble` keys it `<turn>~<deadline_ms>~<hex8>`, `delegation_id` beside it) puts `pending:delegation:<session>#<turn>` at its first curate (`delegation_id`, the round, `until` = the key's deadline, `parked_at` for the sweep). A curate of a duplex call's own turn path while that row stands and `until` is ahead sends no call: its parked turn goes and the mark `turn_parked {delegation_id, iter}` is booked; the delegation answers the turn. A late delegation, one of another turn, an advice round and a text turn change nothing; a duplex turn reads the one row more.

**A tool that failed is never spoken by the quick voice (#1279).** Whether a tool result failed is read off its structure, never its words: the collector parks a result whose header carried an `error_code` with that code on its parked row (never on the turn, which has no key for it) and names it in the `tool_errors` slot of the iteration's curate (`{tool_call_id: error_code}`, this iteration's results only); `./intake` carries the slot unread into the parked turn, and `./policy` reads it there. A result so named, or one whose text is an error object, is a failed tool -- a failure told in prose alone included. After such a result the quick voice gives the round to the brain (`escalate`, reason `unspeakable`), and the brain's window carries ONE turn note `tool_error` (source `curator`, priority 0): "tool not reachable" -- the raw error is never the note.

**Push candidates on a quick way (T4-08).** A candidate came into the window on the ask path of `./push` only, ahead of the round because the round waited for the memory. A routed round (`hop.path`, first assembly) does not wait for it, so `./intake` asks in its first read whether a live candidate stands; if one does and nothing of the turn is in the wall yet, the round stays parked, `./intake` hands `./push` a `push_round` (the round's words, `resume` = its parked key), and `./push` picks with its own rules and answers on `in_addendum` with `resume` -- always, an empty pair included -- without a question to the memory. `./intake` writes the pair and reads the round back behind it, so the pair opens the round. The quick window keeps a candidate pair (`call_candidates_*`) although it drops the memory's rows.

**The window rule (R-IG-1, GH #1085, since 1.11.2).** This hive is the one place that cuts what its model reads, and it cuts by shares of the usable window -- the catalog's `input_soft` the llm cell stamps on every answer, cut to the model's own window alone and kept in state `input_soft` -- turned into characters at 3 per token, never by a typed number: an earlier answer's sidecar block and the collector's sidecar contract (`instructions.sidecar`) 10 %, the summarizer's transcript and the handover note's transcript 50 % of the window of the model that reads them, the summarizer's (state `summarizer_input_soft`: the stamp on each of its answers, the bound of a refusal over its window -- a refused note is measured anew once --, before either `input_soft_fallback`), never the served one, the handover block 50 % (and always under the leaf bound of the llm cell's system gate: without a window the 4 MiB carrier ceiling, with one what the window takes at all), the push candidates' part 5 %, a `history_read` 10 % (a larger block by its id is read in pages, a larger turn range refused with `too_large` and its size), a page of `./reader` 10 % of the asker's window. Without a known window everything goes whole; a find said aloud (`fact`) always does, because the voice model that hears it is not the model this hive serves. The summary has no length of its own: the summarizer is asked to keep it as short as useful and as long as needed, and only a summary whose leaf would not fit the carrier (4 MiB, `CARRIER_MAX_BYTES`) is refused (`too_long: <n> > 4194304 bytes`). Over its share a text carries `...[cut: <shown> of <total> chars shown; <hint>]`, a section or block left out `...[dropped: <name> (<n> chars) over budget]` where it stood -- optional contract sections fall from the back, a required one never, and the contract's frame is written anew over the sections kept, so it never asks for a dropped one -- and every cut is also on the message's hop as `cuts` (`{what, shown, total, unit}`). A shortened block names its whole length (`[#<id>] <first line> [shortened, <shown> of <total> chars — history_read("#<id>")]`). Every ask of a producer (`recall`) carries `input_soft` (tokens, absent when unknown) beside `recall_input_soft`; the parent's edges lift only `recall_input_soft` into the context, so the alias is what the memory hive reads. A pin is refused only over the carrier, one system leaf of at most 4 MiB (`too_long: <n> > 4194304 bytes`); a smaller window's own bound refuses it at the llm cell, with its number. The candidates are switched by `push` `candidate_push` ("1", "0" off).

Numeric knobs take numeric strings; `null` or empty means the default. The word-for-word check (GH #1079) has no knob here: its rule rides on the section's hop, set by the parent's splitter knob `sidecar_verify` (`templates/talky/README.md`).

**A quote at its source (GH #1225).** Beside `turn`, `turn+recall` and `answer`, a field's rule may be `source` (`{"memory":{"items":"facts","quote":"source"}}`; the splitter hands the answer along as `verify_answer` for every rule that names `source`): the item names where its words come from -- `source` `person`, `peer:<ref>` (a bare `<ref>` too) or `agent:<name>`, trimmed and case-folded -- and the quote counts only in the rows of exactly that source: `person` the `user` rows of the turn and of the person's episode the turn answers (`episode_turn_id`: a round of advice or of a timer answers the person's row of its turn); `peer:<ref>` the `peer` rows of that speaker (`speaker_ref`) in the same two places; `agent:cogny` the turn's advice rows, `agent:voice` its delegations, `agent:app:<id>` and `agent:timer:<id>` its rows framed `[app <id>]` / `[timer <id>]` (the frame counts only with an `<id>` in the form of a name, `a-z0-9_.-`, at most 64 characters -- a text that merely starts with `[timer ]` stays the person's; the frame is the sender's, no shipped template sets one yet); `agent:self` the answer (`verify_answer`) and the final answers of the episode. So a round without a person row of its own -- the reasoning core's advice, a delegation, a round an app or a timer opened -- still checks a quote of the person. An item without a source is refused as `source_missing`, one in a form the check does not know as `source_unknown`, a quote not in the rows of exactly its source as `quote_not_in_source`, a failed read of those rows as `store_error`; every refusal leaves a `verify_reject` mark beside its `reject`, and a `topic` mark carries the topic's source. An item is never re-filed under another source. A round an app or a timer opens enters as a user turn whose text starts with its frame (`[app <id>] ...`, `[timer <id>] ...`, id lower-case letters, digits and `_.-`, at most 64); its wall row is kind `app`/`timer`, never `user`, so it never becomes the person's episode.

**Indexes (GH #1225).** Beside `state_key` and `candidates_key` (unique) the ledger declares `slots_path` (unique, above), `wall_episode` (`session_id`, `audience_set`, `episode_idx`, unique) and `pins_round` (`hash`, `source`, `audience_set`, unique) and, not unique, the columns its readers filter and order by: `wall_seq`, `wall_session_turn` (`session_id`, `turn_id`), `wall_session_kind`, `wall_hash`, `blocks_hash`, `marks_kind_seq`, `marks_session_turn`, `calls_call_id`, `summaries_covers`. A store builds them at wake and never drops one; a unique index over rows that collide is not built (one log line) until the copies have healed -- a write of a path replaces all of its rows, `./intake` clears the later copies of an episode index and of a pin once per ledger, across every session (`episode_healed`, stamped in `state` `heal:gh1225`; a child reads the stamp alone first, the pins and the wall only while it is missing), and a pin is written only where none stands -- and the next wake builds it. Existing colonies keep their ledger until re-provisioned: `params` reach a running store only through a new instance.

**The round of a pin (GH #972).** `context.audience_set` of an `in_pin` message is the ceiling: for an app it is its member's round, stamped by the builder's pin edge after every edge of the app. A pin without its own `audience_set` takes the ceiling. A pin that names one keeps it when it is (a) the round of the turn this message belongs to -- `context.turn_round`, stamped once by the edge that raised the turn and never by a config's own edge (the mutation door and the boot pass refuse that as `edge_schema`), so no app can claim it -- or (b) inside the ceiling (narrower, or the ceiling itself). Anything else is not placed and not lost: a stderr line `pin:held source=<s> hash=<12 hex>: <reason>`, and the source pins it again in a turn of that round, whose own stamp is the proof -- nothing is placed out of the ledger. Fail-closed: a chain no turn raised (an app's own timer) has no `turn_round`, so only rounds inside the ceiling are kept; a group goal therefore shows in its group from the next turn of that group on. Since a pin carries its round, every audience group of one source travels in ONE message with ONE `replace_sources`: the replacement ends every live pin of the source in any round except the ones the message carries -- a held pin included, so a turn of another round does not end the group's pin. Without a held pin the replacement is one bundle without a read, and of two replacements in flight the one sent last stands whole. With a held pin `./intake` reads the live set first (`a-live`) and ends only what that read names: two replacements in flight can overtake each other, so a pin of the earlier set that the later one does not carry may stay live until the next replacement; a refused `a-live` ends nothing (stderr) -- the new set stands, the old one ends with the next replacement. Two pins of one text in two rounds are one block and one row (the hash names the text): within one message the first is kept, across messages the later round wins.

**Who a row reaches (GH #925).** Every row of `wall`, `calls`, `marks`, `summaries` and `pins` carries `audience_set`, the canonical JSON array of the round that caused it (`context.audience_set`; a summary and a handover leaf the intersection of their sources and of the round they were made for -- a wider round that joins later does not see them, so whether they appear never hangs on a row it may not see --, a handover note the intersection of every row of its session, so a long session's note may come out narrower than its transcript, never wider; `*` neutral), or `[]` when that round declared none (PP-BD-12, since 1.4.0; NULL only in rows from before the rule, and those reach nobody). A reader -- the window, the handover, the history tools, the recall question, the close batch -- hands a row to a round only when the row's set names `*` or holds the round as a subset (the affinity rule); a NULL row reaches nobody. A round that declares no audience sees the rows of its own session that declare none either, or name `*` -- never a row a declared round said there -- and nothing else, and its call is marked `missing_audience` (a round-less recall question likewise takes no find of a declared gap); each round's clock order carries that round, so a rebuild summarises only what that round may see; a rebuild without a round keeps its plan without a summary (the window shortens, nothing is condensed). A model pin carries the intersection of its `pin` mark and every wall row of its block, NULL when any is. The close batch (`write`) is gated by the close round, `context.audience_set` of `in_close` (the round the generation was opened in): it carries only the rows whose audience holds it, counted in `turn_count`/`round_count`, and a close without a round leaves empty.

## The history tools (`./history`, GH #893)

The model reads its own wall, deterministically and out of this hive's ledger alone: no model, no embedding, and never another model's wall. The schemas are the hive's menu answer (`./schemas` `CURATOR_OFFER`); the answer is a JSON object in the text of one `tool_result`.

| Tool | Arguments | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| `history_search` | `query`; `mode` `phrase` (default: the words in order, any case, any spacing), `exact` (case-sensitive) or `regex` (at most 200 characters); `since`/`until` (ISO-8601, a bare `until` date is the whole day; the time a turn was said, `turn_at` where a replay stamped it, else `at`, GH #1225); `kinds[]`; `limit` 1-20 (8); `context` 0-2 (0) | `hits[]` newest first: `id` (`#<12 hex>`), `seq`, `session_id`, `turn_id`, `at`, `kind`, `excerpt` (120 characters either side of the match), and with `context` the neighbours of the same session as `before`/`after`; `total_hits` (of the rows scanned), `scanned` (rows the round may see), `stopped_at_limit` (the page that filled `limit` was the last one read), `truncated_scan` and `cut_by` (`scan_budget` or `time_budget`) |

**The row bound (GH #925, OR-BD-86).** Rows the round may not see count toward no budget, no page and no neighbour window, but every read stops at twice `scan_budget` rows in all. Where that leaves the answer open -- the visible rows it reached do not settle what the call answers on the same wall without the others -- the call answers in the cut form and shows nothing it read: a search `hits` [], `truncated_scan` true, `stopped_at_limit` false, `cut_by` `scan_budget` and a `detail`, no `scanned` and no `total_hits`; a `history_read` -- a turn range, or a read whose blocks' `release`/`pin` marks reach the bound -- `too_large` with a `detail` and `budget`, no `block_count_at_least`; an outline `sessions` [], `truncated_scan` true and a `detail`. The form is the same on every wall that reaches the bound, so hidden rows move one bit per call -- the bound was reached -- and never a count. It takes at least `scan_budget` hidden rows among the first twice `scan_budget` + 1 rows one read meets (of the `release`/`pin` marks of a read's blocks, more than `scan_budget` among their newest twice `scan_budget`); for the neighbours of a search with `context` (a side whose `context` + 4 nearest rows hold a hidden one is read again, one ledger round trip, the short sides sharing twice `scan_budget` rows) at least `scan_budget` / hits - `context` - 3 hidden rows of the hit's session on one side -- at the defaults with `context` 2: 245 with 20 hits, 620 with 8. Below that no answer shows a hidden row, its id or a word of it, and every number an answer carries counts rows the round may see, never all rows (`scanned`, `total_hits`, `block_count_at_least`, `size`, `turns`, `omitted_sessions`). Metadata channels (GH #932): since 1.4.0 every reader asks the ledger for the rows the round may see (`where audience_set covers <round>`; a round-less read asks for `[]` and `["*"]` rows of its own session), so a hidden row is never read -- page ends, `scanned`, the push and renewal windows and the per-turn `turn_index` hang on visible rows alone, and the row bound and its cut form are a second line that no longer fires on hidden rows. What stays: how long a read takes (the store still walks the hidden rows), the freshness check of a new session's handover, and the counts of `./stats` (OR-BD-6); where a rebuild cuts the window is no longer among them, the plan being one per round since GH #943 (a `release`/`pin` mark acts only on the windows of rounds that may see it and survives every rebuild of another round). `hop.turn_id` of `turn_write` is `<session_id>#<tag>-<index>`, `tag` the first 8 hex of the SHA-256 of the canonical round, the index counted per session and round. The round pays with that call's answer: a narrower `since`/`until` brings it back (a search also a smaller `limit` or no `context`, a range fewer turns, an outline a later `since`).

**The person's last turns (`in_turns_ask`, GH #1304).** `talky/route` reads a turn in the light of the ones before it (its param `context_turns`, OR-EP-4) and asks this hive for them instead of keeping a copy of its own. `./history` answers out of the wall with exactly one `turns`: the person's words alone (rows of kind `user`; no assistant turn, no advice, delegation, peer, app, timer or tool row; several messages of one turn joined by a line break), of the running generation (`context.session_id`; a delta, `context.delta` "1", of any session of the round, live-2 K3) and of the rows the round may see (the gate of `history_read`), never the running turn (`context.turn_id` -- whether it already stands on the wall is a race), the newest `n` oldest first. The texts fit `budget_tokens` by the templates' one token estimate (the block `token-estimate v1`); what does not fit loses its oldest turns first and `truncated` is true. The budget is at most 4000 tokens, and 1500 at or below 0 or without one. A question without `context.audience_set` reads nothing and is answered `turns: []` with `hop.refused` `no_round`; `n` below 1 `bad_arguments`; a ledger read the store refused `turns: []` with `hop.degraded` `store` -- never silence.

**The judge context (#1279).** One structure out of the wall, built when it is asked for -- so it is current after every turn the wall holds, without a timer: `judge = {turns: [{turn_id, role, text}], topic, screen: {topics, state}, now: {iso, weekday, timezone}, place}`, on the turns lane with `hints: [{question, answer, class}]` beside them when there are any (below). `turns` are both roles (`user`, and `assistant` without its sidecar block), one entry per turn and role, oldest first; `topic` the name of the newest named `topic` mark ("" when it ended the topic or there is none); `screen.topics` what `in_screen_topics` holds, `screen.state` `[]`; `now` the clock (`now_utc`) in `timezone`; `place` the knob. `in_turns_ask` with `turns_ask.judge: true` answers `turns` exactly as above and adds `judge`: the running generation's last `n` turns without the running one, within `budget_tokens`, the topic of the generation. Without `judge` (or `false`) the lane reads and answers byte for byte as before. **The person's hints ([#1279](https://github.com/mmeyerlein/meclaw/issues/1279)):** a `curate` that carries the collector's slot `hints` (its `brief_hints` `"1"`: affinity's answer for the round's one person, the entry of `audience_set` that is no `agent:`) is kept by `./intake` at iteration `0` as the state row `judge:hints:<person>`, `{hints, round}` with the round they were brought in (replaced each time, an empty list included; no slot, or a slot whose `hints_of` names somebody else, nothing written; a judge in a round that is no subset of the kept one gets none, R-AF-3), and a judge ask of a round with one person reads that row and adds `judge.hints` when it holds any -- so the stamp of that person's next turn reads how they meant earlier requests, and another person's round reads nothing of it. The seed does not carry them. `in_seed_ask` answers one `seed`: a call that asks for its seed often has no generation yet, so the seed reads the newest rows of the ROUND (the gate of `history_read`, any session, at most 400 rows, a full read leaving its oldest turn out) and the newest named topic of the round. `messages` is one `developer` line of facts (`Facts: now ...; place: ...; topic: ...; screen topics: ...`, only what is there, never an instruction -- the voice cell takes `developer`, `user` and `assistant`), a `developer` line `Notes: <text>; <text>` of the session and until notes the round sees (oldest first; none, no line), the turns as `user`/`assistant` and the round's inner turns (`advice`, `delegation`, `event`) as `developer` lines `(<kind>) <text>` in their place -- `judge.turns` keeps the two roles only --, and the held `in_seed_line`s of the call as the LAST `developer` messages, one per kind in a fixed order -- `hint` first, `wind_down` last, so the end of the call is the last thing the voice model reads; the whole within 8192 tokens by the token estimate, the oldest turns dropped first. Each seed line is dropped once a seed carried it (a delete of each row as it was read, so a line that came in meanwhile waits for the next seed). `est_tokens` is the token estimate the 8192 are counted by (`estimate_tokens`, summed over the messages). The seed writes the facts it carried as `seed_facts:<call>` (`seed_fact_state`: note ids, newest inner turn, topic, screen topics, their digest), the state `./policy`'s `live_feed` compares against; `hop.call_id` is the ask's own (else `context.call_id`). Without `context.audience_set` nothing is read: `messages: []`, `hop.refused` `no_round`; a refused read `hop.degraded` `store` and the line stays -- never silence.
